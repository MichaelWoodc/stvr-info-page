At this point while the benefits of a graywater system are clear, the cost of establishing a passive graywater system in a 
residence is unclear. I am requesting that I be allowed to create a passive graywater system at my home. I will pay for 
the entire system out of my own pocket and I will return the plumbing to any state required by code should this system 
prove unworkable. 
My hope is to create a baseline cost for a residential graywater system that will fall within the 10-year rebate horizon 
of $1826.40. 
Shallow Well Options 
Utilizing shallow wells to water residential landscape areas has been long discussed as a central part of water 
management in the Town of Thunderbolt. The recent challenge regarding this strategy has been the significant 
increases in costs to have these wells drilled commercially.  
In my own situation, I ultimately failed to complete my well by hand due to the density and depth of the dense gray 
clay substrata directly above the subsurface water that often runs 6 to 10 feet deep, depending upon your location. At 
8

 
 
that time, I did turn to a commercial well digging company to complete the project. The price of that time was 
reasonable and given my strong emphasis on conservation practices, I saw this as a reasonable and conservation 
friendly decision on my part. 
Current Cost Challenges 
Recent reports regarding citizens desiring shallow wells have been in the $4000 range. By all estimates, these current 
costs will easily surpass whatever savings one might gain from reduced possible water usage for landscape purposes, 
even if one were to project the savings over a 10-year horizon. As a result, a commercial model for the creation of 
shallow wells locally becomes problematic. 
Past Practices 
In the past, to minimize the costs associated with these projects, there have been groups of three or four neighbors who 
have cooperatively drilled wells by hand and as a result, came up with a very cost-efficient and conservation friendly 
solution to providing water for our yards and gardens. 
Citizens’ Conservation Committee 
One of my thoughts has been the creation of a Citizens’ Conservation Committee that would be made up of interested 
residents of the Town of Thunderbolt who, in conjunction with the ad hoc Committee on Water and Sewer, provide 
feedback to the Council regarding the attitudes of the citizens of Thunderbolt relative to water conservation practices. 
This committee could also be a mechanism whereby citizens could work as a team to create shallow wells for one 
another.  
This could as well be a mechanism for citizens interested in a shallow well for their property, who because of financial 
concerns or physical capacity cannot create a shallow well on their properties by themselves to benefit from the 
community spirit inherent in this type of organization. 
“To Be or Not To Be” 
During the last election cycle, there was a great deal of discussion with regards to creating a greater sense of 
community and what has become a rapidly changing social and physical landscape in the Town of Thunderbolt.  
I consider this an opportunity for the citizens of Thunderbolt, with the support of the Town Council and the town 
administration to advance the concepts of “neighborliness” and “helping out the other guy” as a part of the experience 
of being a resident and citizen and the Town of Thunderbolt.

Lemonade Day is a fun, experiential program that teaches youth how to start, own and operate
their very own business - a lemonade stand. Each year, in participating cities, youth have the
opportunity to experience entrepreneurship by setting up their business during their city's

Lemonade Day is good for kids, good for business and good for Chatham County. Cities and local
businesses within Chatham County are joining forces to bring this innovative program to Thunderbolt and
surrounding communities.
Lemonade Day is a non-profit program dedicated to teaching children how to start, own and operate their
own business through the simple and time-honored act of building and running a lemonade stand.
Lemonade Day provides youth with materials and support free to them, thanks to local sponsors. The
young entrepreneurs who participate in the program earn money and are taught to “spend a little, save a
little and share a little,” donating a portion of their profits to any local charity of their choice.
Lemonade Day is a day of learning and celebrating Thunderbolt’s future. On May 7th, every citizen has a
job-either buying or helping children sell lemonade. The citizens of Thunderbolt are encouraged to simply
purchase a cup of lemonade from one of the youth participating the program to demonstrate how our
community cares for the future of youth. Lowcountry Lemonade Day is a national program presented
locally by Jenny Rutherford Real Estate, who is dedicated to providing an entrepreneurial education
program infused with life skills and character education to our community.
The City of Thunderbolt salutes and commends the sponsors, organizers and participants of this
innovative program, and extends best wishes for a successful and rewarding observance.
Therefore, I, Dana Williams, Mayor of the City of Thunderbolt, hereby proclaim May 7, 2022, as

Proclamation for Sexual Assault Awareness Month 2022 
 
Whereas: The Town of Thunderbolt recognizes that sexual violence has a traumatic effect 
on many of our citizens, including women, men, and children; and 
 
Whereas: Most victims of sexual violence know their assailant and most attacks happen 
in their own home; and 
 
Whereas: Sexual violence is an act of power and intimidation in which the victim feels 
powerless to control what is happening; and 
 
Whereas: Most sexual crimes are not reported because the victims are fearful of 
retribution or fearful of being blamed for the crime against them; and  
 
Whereas: Mary’s Place: Advocacy, Prevention, and Sexual Assault Center of the Coastal 
Empire Inc (formerly the Rape Crisis Center) is one of the partner agencies of the Sexual 
Assault Response Team to address the community’s response to victims who report the 
crime; and  
 
Whereas: Mary’s Place works closely with local hospitals, Chatham County Law 
Enforcement Agencies, and the District Attorney’s office to ensure that victims are 
supported through the court system; and  
 
Whereas: When the COVID-19 pandemic hit in 2019, Mary’s Place staff reworked its 
services to continue to meet the critical needs in our community; and  
 
Whereas: Direct advocacy and support continued, and individual therapy and support 
groups were adjusted; and 
 
Whereas: The prevention programs conducted by Mary’s Place continues both in-person 
and virtually, reaching more than 5,000 people; and 
 
Whereas: To continue to meet the needs of Spanish-speaking survivors in our 
community, Mary’s Place employs a bilingual advocate through funding from the Sexual 
Assault Service Program; and 
 
Whereas: The crisis intervention and prevention programs of the Mary’s Place are 
making a difference in the lives of the residents in the Town of Thunderbolt. 
 
NOW, THEREFORE, I, Dana Williams, Mayor of the Town of Thunderbolt, do hereby 
proclaim the month of April 2022 as: 
 
Sexual Assault Awareness Month 
in Thunderbolt, GA

Town of Thunderbolt Procurement Policy 
 
General 
The intent of this policy is to provide the guidelines for purchasing that will ensure that any 
expenditure of public funds by Town Departments will be consistent with these policies approved 
by the Town Council.  The goals of the organization as well as the overall risk of fraud and/or 
misconduct were the main priorities when designing this purchasing policy.  The Town of 
Thunderbolt is not a large municipality, so many procurement and complex purchasing policies 
are not applicable, or may lead to overcomplicated policies that hard are to follow and only result 
in confusion, frustration and unnecessary paperwork.  When designing this policy, streamlined 
procedures were also taken into consideration without jeopardizing internal controls. 
 
Guidelines 
Delegation of Authority – the Town Administrator shall be responsible for the implementation of 
this policy.  The Town Council shall be notified in writing, via Administrator e-mail, of all 
contracted expenses of $25,000 or greater. 
 
Acceptance of Bids or Quotes – the Town reserves the right to accept a bid, quote or proposal 
which is determined to be the most beneficial to the Town, as well as the right to reject any or 
all bids or quotes.   
 
Contracts – All contracts must be in writing, reviewed and signed off on by the Town Attorney 
and authorized by Town Council with authorization recorded in the minutes of the council 
meeting. 
 
Efficient Purchasing – Every department should strive to purchase the goods and services in the 
appropriate quantities for its foreseeable needs of the department which will maximize any 
possible discounts and ensure an efficient purchasing process that conserves staff time.  
Departments should work together while purchasing.  Specifications and details for Bids and RFPs 
should be established by the respective department and provided to the Town Administrator / 
Designee for organization and publication.  It is not the responsibility of the Town Administrator 
/ Designee to create the specifications for any given department. 
 
Compliance with Adopted Budget – No purchase which would have the ability to exceed the 
Adopted Budget total for the Department may be authorized by a Department Head.  The Town 
Administrator may approve such purchases after notifying the Town Council of the intent and 
the circumstances that require such action, including the funding sources. 
 
Quality – The quality and service of any purchase is just as important as the price.  It is the duty 
of each department to secure the best quality for the purpose intended.  Quality buying is the 
buying of goods and services that will meet the requirements for which they are intended.   
 
 
16

Sales Tax – The Town is generally exempt from paying all local and state sales taxes or Federal 
Excise taxes.  The Town Administrator / Designee can provide any of the necessary exemption 
documentation to vendors upon request.  If the Town purchases property and furnishes that 
property to a contractor for use in a construction, renovation or repair project, the Town will 
provide advance written notice to the contractor of the amount of tax owed for the property and 
that the payment of that tax is the responsibility of the contractor. 
 
Endorsements – Neither the Town’s name, or any employee’s name or position is to be used to 
endorse/support any product or vendor, unless specifically authorized by the Town Council. 
 
Ethics Laws – Public Officials and all employees of the Town are to comply with all federal, state 
and Town ethics laws regarding conflict of interest and purchase decisions.   
 
Purchasing Procedures 
The following procedures have been established, and are subject to certain exceptions and other 
criteria as noted under “Special Requirements” below, in order to regulate the Town’s purchases, 
services, depending on the type and cost of the purchase.  Well maintained and updated 
documentation is to be kept in order to insure that vendors are treated fairly and in the Town’s 
best interests.   
 
Purchases of goods and services, equipment and non-construction capital projects under $15,000 
Department Heads are authorized to make any purchase under $15,000 which is included in the 
annual budget adopted by the Town Council.  Every Department Head may also designate an 
employee, with proper written notice to the Town Administrator for approval, with the authority 
to make such purchases. 
 
Purchases of goods and services from $15,000 to $50,000 
The Town Administrator is authorized to approve any purchases up to $50,000 if the item/service 
has been included in the annual budget adopted by the Town Council.  Prior to the Town 
Administrator approving any purchase from $15,000 to $50,000, the Department Head must 
either (1) obtain three written quotes or (2) explain why three written quotes could or need not 
be obtained.  A summary of the quotes or explanation of why three written quotes was not 
necessary shall be attached to the payment authorization. 
 
The Town Administrator may waive the requirement for three quotations for purchases from the 
State’s cooperative purchasing program and other multi-jurisdictional purchases, and for annual 
professional service consulting contracts for legal, informational technology, financing, auditing, 
engineering services for operational support and for capital projects.   
 
Capital Purchases over $50,000 
The Department / Engineer shall prepare specifications and requirements, as appropriate, in 
order to meet the Town’s capital needs.  These specifications and requirements will be forwarded 
to the Town Administrator & Council for approval.  Upon approval, the Department Head will 
prepare the necessary bid package or request for proposal to provide the Town Administrator / 
17

Town Attorney for review and approval prior to public notice and advertisements, including 
invitation to bid or request for proposal to qualified vendors.  If applicable, the bid or proposal 
shall address anticipated or proposed financing arrangements. 
 
Upon completion of the bidding period, all bids will be opened and tallied, and turned over to 
the Department Head for review. The Department Head will prepare a draft memo to the Town 
Administrator, which will include the Department Head’s recommendation for the bid award.  
Upon approval by the Town Administrator, the bid award shall be presented to the Town Council 
for approval. 
 
The bid award will normally be made to the lowest qualified bidder meeting the specifications 
and requirements, although there may be instances when the lowest bid is not in the best 
interest of the Town.  In these cases it is the responsibility of the Department Head to adequately 
document the reasoning why the lowest bidder should not be selected.   
 
The Town also reserves the right to select or reject a vendor based on the best interest of the 
Town, including but not limited to prior service and experience with a vendor.   
 
Special Requirements 
Sole Source – In the event that there is only one vendor capable of providing a particular good or 
service, then the competitive bid or request for proposal procedures outlined in this policy can 
be waived by the Town Administrator.  When a Department Head determines that goods and 
services must be purchased by a “sole source vendor”, the Department Head shall document in 
writing why only one company or individual is capable of providing the goods or services needed.  
The documentation should be submitted to the Town Administrator for approval.   
 
Cooperative Purchasing Programs – Departments are encouraged to use cooperative purchasing 
programs sponsored by the State or other jurisdictions.  Such programs prove advantageous by 
taking advantage of large quantity purchases made by such cooperatives. 
 
Professional Services – Normal competitive procedures are not ordinarily utilized in securing 
professional services for attorneys, engineers, accountants, planners, and other professional 
people who, in keeping with the standards of their discipline, will not enter into a competitive 
bidding process.  A Request for Proposal (RFP) or Request for Qualification (RFQ), while not 
always required to secure professional services, may be required at the direction of the Town 
Administrator or the Town Council.   
 
These documents shall be submitted to the Town Administrator & Council for approval prior to 
their distribution.  When an RFP/RFQ for professional services has been approved, a limited 
number of qualified professionals known to the Town will be invited to submit a proposal setting 
forth their interest, qualifications and how they can meet the Town’s needs.  In securing 
professional services, it is the primary goal of the Town to obtain the services of professionals 
who have a proven record of providing those services.  A contract will then be negotiated with 
the professional that has best met the needs of the Town.  Contracts for legal, auditing, financial, 
18

technology, compliance and general engineering services may be made for one year with annual 
extension or on a continuous basis until terminated. 
 
Emergency Purchases – The procedures in this policy may be waived by the Town Administrator 
under emergency conditions when a delay in the purchase will threaten the basic mission of a 
department.  Emergency conditions are considered unforeseen circumstances, where the need 
for immediate corrective action is absolutely necessary.   
 
Petty Cash Accounts – A petty cash fund has been established for emergency purchases under 
$50.  Any purchase over $50 must follow regular purchasing procedures.  The petty cash fund will 
be managed by the Town Administrator / Designee, reconciled and replenished on a monthly 
basis.  Receipts must be maintained for all purchases and included in monthly reconciliation. 
 
Town Credit Cards – Town credit cards may be issued to Department Heads as approved by the 
Town Administrator for purchases not to exceed $5,000.  The responsible Department Head shall 
attach receipts to the credit card bill for all charges.  Failure to attach receipts for all purchases 
shall result in the Finance Administrator rescinding the Department’s use of the credit card.   
 
The use of the Town credit card(s) for personal use, even for a very short period of time, is strictly 
prohibited and may be grounds for disciplinary action. 
 
Specifications  
When goods and services are to be purchased through the competitive process detailed in this 
policy, specifications (specs) must be prepared to meet the following goals: 
 
• Identify the minimum requirements needed 
• Allow for a competitive bid or quote, when possible 
• Be capable of objective review 
• Provide for an equitable award at the lowest possible cost 
 
General Guidelines – Specifications should be kept in a manner that is as simple as possible while 
still allowing for the exactness required in order to keep bidders from utilizing loopholes to avoid 
providing the quality desired or take advantage of their competitors.  To promote the best 
possible competition for a product or service, specifications should be written in clear simple 
language, free of vagueness or anything that would be subject to variations based on 
interpretations.   
 
If the specifications include optional goods or services, they must be defined and identified 
separately so that the base cost can be clearly determined compared to the cost(s) of the options.  
If the options are included, the Bid Tabulation Report will have to identify the different costs of 
the options.

Leases 
In some circumstances, tax-exempt leasing serves as an alternative to issuing municipal bonds or 
using current revenues to finance large projects or purchases.  When considering a capital lease 
alternative, the following factors and guidelines should be adhered to: 
1. Determine the future financial impacts lease payments are on an annual basis, including 
the term of the lease.  The term of the lease should not exceed the useful life of the asset.   
2. Determine the necessity for the proposed project / purchase.  The necessity for the 
project / purchase, rather than the expediency of its financing, should justify the funding 
decision. 
3. Evaluate the cost-effectiveness of tax-exempt leasing.  We should not lease small 
equipment out of habit.  By paying for certain equipment items out of current revenues, 
we avoid interest charges all together.   
4. When tax-exempt leasing is preferable, unless determined to be financing unfavorable, 
the Town will use a master lease through GMA.   
5. All leases must be handled in the same manner, with same requirements, as above 
purchasing guidelines. 
6. Lease agreement must be reviewed and approved by Town Administrator and Town 
Attorney prior to execution.

Council.  All checks over $15,000 must have the signature of the Town Administrator.

on a monthly basis for review.

Service Level Agreement 
 
Maximum Response Times via Severity Level 
• 
1 hour for crisis issues 
• 
4-6 hours for critical issues    
• 
24 hours for normal issues 
 
Crisis issues are defined as when a website error renders the CMS program or website completely unusable or 
nearly unusable or introduces a high degree of operational risk and no workaround is available. Till this every 
error is resolved, the website is essentially halted. A large number of users and or core program functionality a 
severely impacted.  
 
Critical issues are defined as website errors that are an inconvenience or causes a consistent behavior of the 
website, which does not impede the normal functioning of the website. It could be an error that occurs 
consistently and affects non-essential functions and is an inconvenience which impacts a small number of users. 
May also contain visual errors for the graphical display of the website that is not ideal but still functioning 
correctly. 
 
Normal issues are defined as an error that has a small degree of significance or is a minor cosmetic issue, or is a 
one-off case. A one-off case occurs when the error occurs and cannot be reproduced easily. These are errors that 
do not impact the daily use of the website. A low error is something that does not affect normal use, and can be 
accepted for a period of time, but user would eventually want changed.

POLICE DEPARTMENT: 
 
 
During the month of March, officers made four drug arrests while doing traffic 
enforcement.  I had a citizen tell me how we had some really nice officers on our 
department.  He said his wife was at the car wash on Whitmarsh Island and she had 
their two children with her.  The citizen said the officer went over and handed his two 
children a Thunderbolt Police Department sticker badge and that it absolutely made 
their day, and was very thankful for that encounter.  All of the officers are almost 
finished with their required training for the year and Officer Jones just completed his 
last block of training for his Supervision certification.   
 
I attended a local District 6 meeting and got information on an upcoming Professional 
Management Program (PMP) which is provided by Columbus State University.  I have 
one officer interested in attending, and the Hope Grant will pay for it.  The class design 
is set up where the student will attend, 10, 40-hour classes, that are spread out over the 
course of about a year and half.  After the students receive their PMP certification, they 
will also be able to obtain their POST Supervisory, and Managerial certifications, plus 
they will also receive 30 hours of college credit.  This is a great program, and I’m 
excited to be able to send someone from this department.
