Council Member Drohan spoke a brief overview.  The River Drive District was developed out of an understanding that 
the properties along River Drive were substantially increasing in value and as a result were becoming less and less 
likely to be utilized as residential properties. The understanding at the time was that the lack of continuity in zoning 
along River Drive along with the lack of standards relative to the future development of the significant number of 
undeveloped properties in that area required attention by the town Council and Planning and Zoning committee. There 
was significant effort put forth in order to achieve both the continuity in zoning along River Drive as well as clarifying 
the standards for the future development of the properties in that zone.  Furthermore, there was a notable determination 
made when considering those properties bordering on both River Drive and Bannon Drive regarding the nature of the 
development of those properties. Specifically, it was determined that future development of those properties bordering 
on Bannon Drive would reflect the residential quality of those properties directly across the road from the newly 
developed River Drive district. The discussion at the time reflected a desire that differently zoned properties across a 
common roadway would be undesirable and that the property rights the owners could be negatively affected unless we 
made this clear in the ordinance.  Council Member Drohan cited the height restrictions.

written to specifically address future development of properties in this zoning district so that future development of

{FROM THE ORDINANCE} 
“It is the intent of this section to require all properties and structures within the River Drive District to comply with 
the River Drive District standards over time so that the district will at some point have a design and appearance 
consistent with the intent of the general principles set forth in this ordinance.”  This portion of the ordinance 
emphasizes the intent of the ordinance to bring about the structural, visual and functional continuity in the River Drive 
District over time.  The 5 lots in question left a de facto “spot zoning” situation that was created by the Council at the 
time.  By all traditional zoning standards, these situations make for future challenges that often lead to unintended 
negative consequences.  This correction will diminish the potential for such unintended consequences in the future and 
help to fulfill the goal of the River Drive District ordinance as it was originally conceived. 
There were concerns voiced during the review of this proposal at the planning and zoning committee regarding 
maintaining Thunderbolt as a “small town” and that should these parcels become businesses in the future that traffic 
will increase and add to the current traffic issues. 
The River Drive district specifically states the following: 
“In order to foster activity and vibrancy along River Drive, the ground floor of buildings fronting on River Drive 
shall incorporate a publicly-accessible, active use on the ground floor.” 
This portion of the ordinance emphasizes the intent of the River Drive District to recognize that River Drive, by 
virtue of the changes in land value, utilization options and concerns with regards to inadequate planning, will indeed 
likely become more highly utilized as vacant properties are developed and that, in the face of a narrowing “window 
of opportunity”, the Council chose to move forward to establish for the town a functional model for development for 
River Drive. 
{FROM THE ORDINANCE} 
“I. Intent. River Drive is the main point of entry into Thunderbolt and serves as the front door and "main street" of 
Thunderbolt through the civic presence of town hall and Waterfront Park, commercial presence of restaurants and 
businesses, as well as the front door to the Wilmington River/Intracoastal Waterway. As such, the development 
along River Drive should reflect the town's unique character, including its cultural and architectural heritage.” 
This section emphasizes the importance of River Drive as our town center and as such the natural location for 
commercial development. It also emphasizes the intent of the ordinance to be certain that all the development along 
River Drive reflects “the town’s unique character, including its cultural and architectural heritage.” 
{FROM THE ORDINANCE} 
II. Guiding Principles. 
“The following principles shall guide development and redevelopment along River Drive: 
1.Public Waterfront—The waterfront and marshes are inherently tied to the culture, heritage, and economic 
prosperity of Thunderbolt. Despite previous decisions, visual connections and, where feasible, physical connections 
to the river and marsh are of the utmost importance.” 
5

 
At the time, this discussion revolved around the poor and inadequate considerations regarding development along 
River Drive that occurred many years ago resulting in the loss of visual access to the Riverfront and the marshes 
that historically was the domain of River Drive. 
{FROM THE ORDINANCE} 
“2.Town—Thunderbolt is a town—larger than a village, but smaller than a city. The scale and design of the 
buildings, the landscape, and hardscape shall reflect the intimate ambiance and character of Thunderbolt.” 
 
This portion of the ordinance recognizes Thunderbolt for what it is, a town, and emphasizes the importance of 
continuity surrounding development for the sake of securing the visual and structural connection with our history. 
{FROM THE ORDINANCE} 
“3.Tree Canopy—One of the most iconic images of Thunderbolt is the "tree island" in River Drive with majestic 
live oaks forming a canopy over River Drive. Fortunately, many other live oaks exist along River Drive as well. 
Whenever possible, healthy existing live oaks should be preserved and new ones planted to sustain the canopy over 
time.” 
Here again, the importance of tradition is recognized in the ordinance even as a relates to our “tree architecture”, 
which in my estimation is unmatched in the region. Protecting and sustaining this traditional component of our town 
again requires continuity of regulation and application along River Drive. 
{FROM THE ORDINANCE} 
“4. A Place for All—As the "Main Street," River Drive should comfortably accommodate people of all ages, 
abilities, and mobility choices. The public realm and the adjacent development, shall provide a comfortable and 
inviting walking environment through appropriate design, scale, infrastructure, and landscape.” 
Finally, the town Council at the time recognized the importance of creating a district that valued the traditions of  
“a stroll downtown” alongside the realities of the predictable shifts in land use along River Drive and appreciating 
our mutual responsibilities allowing for safety and access for all citizens and visitors who choose to enjoy the 
wonders of Thunderbolt. Again, zoning continuity along River Drive is central to the achievement of this ideal as 
put forth in the River Drive District ordinance. 
Concern #2 Traffic/Parking 
It is clear in the River Drive District ordinance that specific attention was paid to issues of traffic and parking and 
with an appreciation that without appropriate standards of development that the Town of Thunderbolt risks 
becoming a hodgepodge of development that would certainly detract from the inherent character and beauty of the 
town. 
Concerns with regards to the effect of police stops are valid but actually exists under the current situation. The 
continuity of development along the River Drive District enhances the ability for input from public safety to assure 
the safe and efficient management of traffic. 
Zoning continuity is consistent with addressing these issues as the development standards inherent in the zoning 
provides for planful consideration of future development. 
Concern #3

Prior positions of property owners regarding rezoning 
As I understand it, there was concern that certain property owners among the properties currently under 
consideration had objected to an R-3 zoning change at some point in the distant past. I say this as no such request

reasonably so, that this may relate to a prior request to extend the disastrous “Great Wall of Thunderbolt” beyond its 
current extent, a choice that I would consider fatal to the character of Thunderbolt. 
 
The River Drive district was designed to maximize physical and visual access to the Wilmington River and the 
marshes for the benefit of many as opposed to the few. As such, the River Drive district has in fact enhanced the 
unique character of the Town of Thunderbolt for the future and the inclusion of those properties currently under 
discussion enhances the capacity of the town to develop in a responsible and aesthetically pleasing manner.

a) Council Meeting Structure Changes 
 
Mayor Williams stated the way the Town runs their meetings is antiquated and asked Council Member Drohan 
to read the changes. 
 
9

 
As our monthly Town Council meetings are designed for the Town Council to conduct business and review 
Town ongoings to include financials, projects, direction, legislation etc.… The Town Council finds that to 
ensure best services and to operate in a transparent environment that is more consistent with best practices as 
recognized nationally and by Georgia Municipal Association, the following modifications shall apply.  
 
Be it moved that beginning on June 8th 2022 that the rules related to the structure and conduct of the monthly 
Thunderbolt Town Council meetings be adapted as follows: 
 
1- Open Workshop – Town Council will begin at the same time of 6:30 p.m., but an open workshop will begin at 
6:00 p.m. to ensure that any affected or interested parties may have time to clarify or understand the matters to 
be discussed or voted on listed on the agenda. The workshop will be the time for citizen input, questions, etc., 
as related to agenda items only.  The chairman of the meeting will have the ability to limit speakers to no less 
than three minutes per speaker if time constraints arise.  Concurrently, the chairman may elect to delay the 
start of regular session based on the needs of the workshop.  
Per the open meetings act, an item not on the agenda may be added only if it arises by unforeseen 
happenstance during the meeting.  If a non-agenda item is added within the meeting, the chairman will allow 
affected individuals to speak, provide input, or questions regarding only the topic at hand and each speaker 
will be provided two minutes maximum.  Concurrently, the chairman may elect to ensure individuals on both 
sides of an issue are heard and then close the public comment section, meaning not every desiring individual 
may have the ability to speak.  
2- That the second portion of the meeting will be designated as the “Council Business Meeting” which will be the 
opportunity for members of Council to consider and vote on those items that are on the current agenda. In 
accordance with the ordinances guiding this portion of the meeting, agenda items will be heard, motions may 
be made and seconded, discussion among members of Council in front of the public can take place and voting 
for the record can occur. Any Councilmember may request to the chairman to seek further input from the 
public during this portion of the meeting. 
3- That following the Council Business Meeting will be the “Mayor and Council” portion of the meeting, 
wherein all members of Council will continue to retain the right to present and discuss those issues they deem 
of importance. This should include the option for members of Council to request input from the public. Again, 
in keeping with Open Meetings Laws the items that Mayor and Council desire to discuss should be provided 
previously to the Town Clerk to be included in the meeting agenda.  This will allow for the public to ask 
questions and provide input in the workshop relative to item one.  Again, open forum exists for the unforeseen 
matters that came about during the course of the meeting.  
4- That following the Mayor and Council portion of the meeting, the Council may at its discretion adjourn from 
the open meeting into “Executive Session” for the discussion of appropriate legal and personnel discussions 
and the purchase, sale or lease of property. 
5- Following executive session, Council will reconvene into regular session, and end the meeting if no further 
business.  
6- The Town will ask that no more than three members of council, and the Town Administrator, if possible, stay 
behind for fifteen minutes to discuss any topics that were discussed at the meeting, or thoughts, needs, or 
recommendations they may have for the Town. This is not a requirement, but an avenue to ensure some face 
time with the citizens that may wish to address a new issue, or clarify a previously discussed item.

American Rescue Plan Act 
State and Local Fiscal Recovery Funds (SLFRF) 
 
The Town of Thunderbolt received a grant award of $489,762 in July 2021 and the funds are accounted 
for in the Town’s ARPA Fund.  A second payment of $489,762 is expected to be received in 2022, for a 
total grant award of $979,524.  The funds are required to be obligated by December 31, 2024 and 
expended by December 31, 2026. 
The U.S. Department of the Treasury requested all non-entitlement unit (NEU – population less than 
50,000) cities that received awards under $10 million to select the revenue replacement standard 
allowance in their Project and Expenditure report that was due on April 30, 2022. This option was 
created specifically for small municipalities to streamline and simplify the reporting process significantly 
and the process does not require any bulk reporting uploads. The Town submitted the first project and 
expenditure report on April 25, 2022. 
The Town will spend the award by offsetting general government services salaries and wages 
expenditures with the funds received under the SLFRF award.  The amount held in the ARPA Fund can be 
transferred to another Town Fund for desired projects at the discretion of the Council. The 
recommendation is to fully expend the funds on salaries, then utilize the funds as needed or for reserves 
that are pre-budgeted from the tax base.
