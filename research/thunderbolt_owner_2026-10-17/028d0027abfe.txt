6:00 PM 
 
 
 
AGENDA HEARING 
 
Public has five minutes per person to ask questions or comment on any agenda item. 
Mayor Williams opened the public hearing up at 6 pm. 
 
Beth Goette, 3006 Leonard St., asked for an explanation of the Owner-Occupied Amendment to the STR Ordinance. 
Attorney Barrow explained the change to Ms. Goette. Council Member Crenshaw stated the Town could require proof 
such as a Homestead Exemption to prove owner resides on the property.  
 
Council Member Crenshaw stated the Town should require signage with a list of contact numbers, even if it is 9-1-1 
listed. Attorney Barrow stated there was a big debate regarding signage and Council decided on the Ordinance as it is 
currently written. Council Member Drohan stated the sign issue was regarding size of signage.  
 
Beth Goette stated a Homestead Exemption is for primary residence. Attorney Barrow stated that is just one factor. 
Council Member Barbaree stated Stephens-Day Exemption is another.  
 
Council Member Crenshaw commented that Tybee is trying to pull back on the number of STRs allowed.  
 
Mayor Williams closed the public comment at 6:29 pm.  
 
REGULAR MEETING 
 
1. Call to order - Mayor Williams called the meeting to order at 6:30 pm.  
 
2. Roll Call by Presiding Officer with Invocation, followed by the Pledge of Allegiance  
 
In Attendance is Mayor Dana Williams and Council Members: Larry Ward, Bethany Skipper-Greer, Edward 
Drohan, James Lavin, and Brooks Barbaree.  Staff in attendance: Police Chief, Sean Clayton, Police Lieutenant, 
Brandon Runyon; Fire Chief, Andrew Bateman; Public Works representative, Ben Oliver; Town Attorney, Charlie 
Barrow; Accounts Payable Manager, Katina Spaulding; Utility Clerk, Renee Holmes; Town Clerk/Zoning 
Administrator, Matthew Walker. 
  
3. Approval of Amended Meeting Agenda (moving Consent Agenda items C, D, and F to discussion agenda) 
 
Mayor Williams asked for a motion to approve the amended agenda. Council Member Ward made the motion 
to approve with a second by Council Member Crenshaw.  The motion passed unanimously. 
 
 
 

 
4. Approval of Minutes from Previous Meeting: 
 
Minutes of May 8, 2024 
 
Mayor Williams asked for a motion to approve the meeting minutes of May 8, 2024.  Council Member Ward 
made the motion to approve with meeting minutes May 8, 2024. Council Member Crenshaw provided the 
second. The motions passed unanimously. 
 
5. Presentation: 
 
a) William Gettis with Savannah Interagency Diversity Council presented Mayor Williams with a plaque of 
appreciation for attending and being a part of the Traffick Jam to fight against human trafficking.  
 
b) Mayor Williams read the “Know Local-Go Local” business spotlight for July 2024. The businesses 
recognized in July are: Terracon, Victory Drive Self Storage, Jones Street Productions, and Erica Davis 
Lowcountry.  
 
6. Finance Report: 
 
There was no Financial Report due to the council meeting being moved to an earlier date in June.  
 
7. Consent Agenda: 
 
a) Approval of Budget amendment for 2023 
 
 
Shannon Brett of HBBB explained the need for the budget amendment.  
 
 
 
 
 
b) Approval of ARPA Fund budget amendment and Resolution  
 
 
 
 
 
 
Shannon Brett of HBBB explained ARPA to Council Member Lavin.  
 
c) First Reading of STR Ordinance Amendment  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Re: Definition of “Owner-Occupied” 
 
d) First Reading of Fence Ordinance Amendment 
 
Council Member Lavin stated he read the amendment and questioned why it was being changed. Council 
Member Drohan stated the fence application from a few months ago led to this discussion. Originally, the 
ordinance was written for the standard 60’ x 100’ lot with 25’ setbacks. There are non-traditional lots 
along Bonaventure Road and the Amendment also considers the character of the neighborhood.  
 
 
 
 
A motion was made by Council Member Ward to approve all items of the amended consent agenda. 
Council Member Crenshaw provided the second. The motion passed unanimously.  
 
8. Discussion Agenda: 
 
a) Consideration to approve CORE MPO Memorandum of Understanding 
 
 
Council Member Drohan stated his problem is there is minimal impact on Thunderbolt. He has an issue 
paying what isn’t being impacted. Council Member Drohan stated there has been a massive increase in 
traffic and the heavy intersections of Shell Rd. and Sunset Blvd. Both start in Thunderbolt and goes to 
Savannah. There is a need for controlled intersections and the MPO should help us with regional traffic 
growth. Council Member Ward stated 2 synchronized lights would save the problem at Shell Rd.  
 
 
 

 
A motion was made by Council Member Drohan to approve CORE MPO Memorandum of 
Understanding. Council Member Ward provided the second. The motion passed unanimously. 
 
b) Second Reading and consideration to approve Ordinance to Amend Zoning Map 
 
 
Council Member Drohan stated the Shell Road corridor is underused and undertaxed, mostly bought at tax 
sales. He stated the owner of MBS Properties has good ideas. Council Member Drohan feels the whole 
zone needs to be looked at by Planning and Zoning in regards to the comp plan. He is open to other 
property owners coming forward for zoning.  
 
Council Member Lavin asked how it impacted the area and other property owners. Council Member Ward 
suggested more information regarding the position of other residents in the area and the study completed 
by Symbiocity, similar to what the Town did for the River Drive District and the Victory Drive District. 
 
A motion was made by Council Member Drohan to approve Ordinance to Amend Zoning Map and 
request study of impact to the Shell Road corridor. Council Member Ward provided the second. 
The motion passed unanimously. 
 
c) Second Reading and Consideration to Approve STR Ordinance Amendment  
 
 
Re: Change in ownership  
 
 
A motion was made by Council Member Ward to approve STR Ordinance Amendment regarding 
Change in Ownership. Council Member Drohan provided the second. The motion passed 
unanimously. 
 
9. Department Report – Written Reports Submitted 
 
by:   
Police / Municipal Court / Neighborhood Watch – Sean Clayton, Chief of Police 
Chief Clayton had nothing to add to his report. Council Member Lavin asked about the speed cameras 
in Town. Chief Clayton explained the Sunset Blvd camera is a 65/35 split and the Shell Rd camera is a 
35/65 split. Chief Clayton added the speed indicators are up and running. The Flock cameras have also 
been productive and helpful. Two stolen vehicles were located, a felony arrest was made, and a 
warrant service was conducted due to the help of the Flock cameras.  
 
Fire –Fire Chief, Andrew Bateman   
Chief Bateman had nothing to add to his report. Chief Bateman stated the FD radios should be shipped 
within 2 weeks.  
 
Public Works Department – Ben Oliver, Public Works  
Ben Oliver stated that due to the earlier date of the June 2024 meeting, a report is not available. 
 
Town Clerk – Matthew Walker, Town Clerk 
Clerk Walker had nothing further to add to his report.

Nothing to report or discuss

Anna Maria Thomas discussed the Flea Markets will start back in September. The museum is taking donations for 
items in the museum store.

Council Member Ward made a motion at 7:25 pm to leave regular session to enter executive session to

Mayor's "Know Local- Go Local" Initiative 
Thunderbolt Businesses 
Spotlight Recognition 
For August 2024 
WHEREAS, The Mayor's "Know Local-Go Local" Business Spotlight Program was created in 
order to recognize and showcase local area businesses that are vital to the Town's identify, one of 
small-Town charm, and local business that serve and support families in our community. 
This recognition program is exclusive to none, for we all stand to benefit as a community via 
prosperity of our Town's businesses. Through this program, members of the community are 
exposed to a variety of businesses that have displayed their desire to be excellent stewards of not 
only their business, but of the community as a whole, ensured to support diversity in their 
workplace, and a desire to continue to do the excellent work, service, or craft they perform. 
It is with the forementioned values at the ready, I Mayor Dana Williams proudly recognize and 
encourage all our citizens and visitors to visit these establishments, learn about them, view their 
websites, social media, and ultimately support these and all local businesses. 
Hereby the following is recognized as the Thunderbolt Business Spotlight of the week... 
 
 
The week of August 4th, 2024- "Know Local- Go Local" Spotlight is... 
 
Intracoastal Concrete Construction – Concrete Construction Specialists 
 
 
The week of August 11th, 2024- "Know Local- Go Local" Spotlight is... 
 
Sunrise Healing Arts – Massage Therapy and Wellness 
 
 
The week of August 18th, 2024- "Know Local-Go Local" Spotlight is... 
 
Turner Food and Spirits – Award-winning restaurant group  
 
 
The week of August 25th, 2024 - "Know Local - Go Local" Spotlight is... 
 
Roger’s Package Shop – Beer, Wine, and Spirits Store  
 
 
I and We look forward to spotlighting all the businesses within the Town of Thunderbolt, as we 
continue to grow and develop as a Town and Community to support one another. 












 
 
Amendment to Section 6 – 301 of the ordinances of the Town of 
Thunderbolt. 
 
Section 6 – 301 (b) of the ordinances of the Town of Thunderbolt is hereby 
amended by adding thereto a new paragraph to add a definition for 
“Owner-Occupied” to read as follows: 
 
“Owner-occupied property shall mean real property which contains one to 
four dwelling unit(s) in one structure where the principal dwelling unit 
must be occupied by the property owner and constitute his/her primary 
and usual place of residence. The dwelling units must share the Property 
Identification Number assigned by the Chatham County Board of 
Assessors.”  
 
So adopted by the Mayor and Council of the Town of Thunderbolt on this 
the ________ day of _______2024.

POLICE DEPARTMENT: 
 
I went to the District 6 meeting and Garden City P.D. said we can use their firearms 
simulator any time we need.  We will be scheduling officers randomly throughout the 
year for simulator training.  This type of training is designed to not only increase 
efficiency, but it also increases accuracy, reaction times and consistency. 
 
Port Wentworth P.D. contacted our officers with helping them locate a suspect that they 
had charges for, who supposedly resided in Thunderbolt.  Thunderbolt Officers located 
and took that suspect into custody, and it was later discovered that, the same suspect 
was also wanted by the Effingham County Sheriff’s office as well. 
 
The suspect who stole a vehicle out of Laurel Oak Lane and committed an aggravated 
assault in Savannah’s jurisdiction was identified by Savannah’s major crimes unit.  A 
detective with Savannah made contact with our officers revealing that suspect’s 
identity, so now we have pending charges on that suspect for the theft of that 
automobile as well as other charges related to that crime.
