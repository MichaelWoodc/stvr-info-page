At this point while the benefits of a graywater system are clear, the cost of establishing a passive graywater system in a 
residence is unclear. I am requesting that I be allowed to create a passive graywater system at my home. I will pay for 
the entire system out of my own pocket and I will return the plumbing to any state required by code should this system 
prove unworkable. 
My hope is to create a baseline cost for a residential graywater system that will fall within the 10-year rebate horizon 
of $1826.40. 
Shallow Well Options 
Utilizing shallow wells to water residential landscape areas has been long discussed as a central part of water 
management in the Town of Thunderbolt. The recent challenge regarding this strategy has been the significant 
increases in costs to have these wells drilled commercially.  
In my own situation, I ultimately failed to complete my well by hand due to the density and depth of the dense gray 
clay substrata directly above the subsurface water that often runs 6 to 10 feet deep, depending upon your location. At 
that time, I did turn to a commercial well digging company to complete the project. The price of that time was 
reasonable and given my strong emphasis on conservation practices, I saw this as a reasonable and conservation 
friendly decision on my part. 
Current Cost Challenges 
Recent reports regarding citizens desiring shallow wells have been in the $4000 range. By all estimates, these current 
costs will easily surpass whatever savings one might gain from reduced possible water usage for landscape purposes, 
even if one were to project the savings over a 10-year horizon. As a result, a commercial model for the creation of 
shallow wells locally becomes problematic. 
Past Practices 
In the past, to minimize the costs associated with these projects, there have been groups of three or four neighbors who 
have cooperatively drilled wells by hand and as a result, came up with a very cost-efficient and conservation friendly 
solution to providing water for our yards and gardens. 

 
Citizens’ Conservation Committee 
One of my thoughts has been the creation of a Citizens’ Conservation Committee that would be made up of interested 
residents of the Town of Thunderbolt who, in conjunction with the ad hoc Committee on Water and Sewer, provide 
feedback to the Council regarding the attitudes of the citizens of Thunderbolt relative to water conservation practices. 
This committee could also be a mechanism whereby citizens could work as a team to create shallow wells for one 
another.  
This could as well be a mechanism for citizens interested in a shallow well for their property, who because of financial 
concerns or physical capacity cannot create a shallow well on their properties by themselves to benefit from the 
community spirit inherent in this type of organization. 
“To Be or Not To Be” 
During the last election cycle, there was a great deal of discussion with regards to creating a greater sense of 
community and what has become a rapidly changing social and physical landscape in the Town of Thunderbolt.  
I consider this an opportunity for the citizens of Thunderbolt, with the support of the Town Council and the town 
administration to advance the concepts of “neighborliness” and “helping out the other guy” as a part of the experience 
of being a resident and citizen and the Town of Thunderbolt.
