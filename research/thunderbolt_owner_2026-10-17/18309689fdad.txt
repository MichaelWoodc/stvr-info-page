Council Member Drohan presentation –  
The purpose of this project is to evaluate the various challenges related to infrastructure relative to potential future 
development within the Town of Thunderbolt. The primary goal is to be able to provide to the Town Council the 
pertinent data required to formulate policy that responsibly manages resources, infrastructure and development 
throughout the town respecting the rights of property owners and the general citizenry relative to fairness and quality 
of life as these might be impacted by future development. 
Development Districts (DD) were assigned for the purpose of data collection as follows: 
-River Dr DD

Scoring- An Example 
A central issue in this research relates to sewer resources and how it can fairly be shared among the stakeholders, 
citizens and developers. I suggest that we use a fairly simple analytical model for this purpose. 
Daily Limits  
minus Buffer for Emergencies =  
Rational Daily Sewer Resources  
 
Rational Daily Sewer Resources  
minus Current Average Sewer Utilization = Available Surplus Daily Sewer Resources 
Each property is then scored based on its current zoning status for the number of housing units based an assumption of 
2.75 occupants per unit. 
These scored values will then be totaled across each District and then Town-wide to provide us with the Potential 
Sewer Resource Utilization. 
Available Surplus Daily Sewer Resources minus  
Potential Sewer Resource Utilization = 
Projected Sewer Resource Availability. 
Projected Sewer Resource Availability 
provides us with an important but only one of the necessary measures Council will need to make create responsible, 
data driven policy relative to future development in Thunderbolt. 
Data collection for the TDRUP has begun and is currently ongoing. Oscar, Deatre and Bob have all taken on 
responsibilities in that regard and the hope that this project can be completed in the next 30-45 days. 
The results hopefully will provide the Council with solid information regarding the complex process of policy 
development relative to land development in a rapidly changing financial and utilization landscape.

Initiative: 
Explore a 90-day pilot to determine if compost service for the community of 
Thunderbolt will impact the water usage of the residents and deter food items from 
causing stress on the pipes. This exploration will help determine feasibility on a large 
scale, costs or savings associated, and possible preventative support for future 
maintenance. 
Scope of Work: 
COR Compost will provide weekly or Bi-weekly services for the select group of

1.0 SERVICES 
 
1.1 Scope of Contracted Services: 
The Contractor shall provide all expertise, personnel, tools, materials, equipment, 
transportation, supervision and all other services and facilities of any nature 
necessary to execute, complete and deliver the timely removal and lawful disposal of 
all eligible storm-generated debris (herein referred to as “debris”), including 
hazardous and industrial waste materials and within the time specified in this 
Contract.  Emergency push, debris removal and demolition of structures will be 
limited to: 1) that which is determined to eliminate immediate threats to life, public 
health, and safety; 2) that which has been determined to eliminate immediate threats 
of significant damage to improved public or private property; and 3) that which is 
considered essential to ensure the economic recovery of the affected community to 
the benefit of the community at large.

1.3 Right-of-Way (ROW) Removal: 
The Contractor shall remove all debris from the ROW of the Town when directed to

5.0 GENERAL TERMS AND CONDITIONS 
 
5.1 Geographic Assignment:

The Contractor shall make scheduled passes at the direction of the Town and/or 
unscheduled passes of each area impacted by the storm event.  The Town shall 
direct the interval timing of all passes.  Sufficient time shall be permitted between

5.3 Operation of Equipment: 
The Contractor shall operate all trucks, trailers and all other equipment in compliance 
with any/all applicable federal, state and local rules and regulations.  Equipment shall 
be in good working condition.  All loading equipment shall be operated from the road, 
street or ROW using buckets and/or boom and grapple devices to collect and load 
debris.  No equipment shall be allowed behind the curb or outside of the public ROW 
unless otherwise directed by the Town.  Should operation of equipment be required 
outside of the public ROW, the Town will provide a Right-of-Entry Agreement, as set 
out in Section 1.6 of this Contract. 
 
5.4 Certification of Load Carrying Capacity: 
The Contractor shall provide for each piece of hauling equipment a certified report 
such as a registration indicating the type of vehicle, make and model, year, license 
plate number, and/or trailer VIN number. Each operator will be required to maintain 
an appropriating operating license and insurance on each road worthy vehicle. 
 
The measured volume of each piece of equipment shall be calculated from the actual 
physical measurement of the container as determined by the Town, its monitoring 
firm, and Contractor Representative(s).  Contractors will be required to acknowledge 
the volume assigned and raise any disputes prior to completion of the certification 
process.  Upon completion of the certification process each certified container will 
receive a unique certification ID number. 
 
5.5 Vehicle Information: 
The maximum load capacity of each hauling vehicle will be rounded to the nearest 
tenth of a cubic yard (CY).  The measured maximum load capacity (as adjusted) of 
any vehicle container will be the same as shown on the placard provided by the 
Contractor and placed on the driver’s side of the container nearest as possible to the 
cab door.  If necessary, the Contractor may be required to provide placards for both

The Contractor shall be responsible for the security of debris on/in each vehicle or 
piece of equipment utilized to haul debris.  Prior to leaving the loading site(s), the 
Contractor shall ensure that each load is secure and trimmed so that no debris

fallen or blown debris from the roadway(s).

The Contractor is responsible for establishing and maintaining appropriate traffic

disposal site(s). 
 
5.8 Work Days/Hours:

operations may be conducted 24 hours a day, seven days per week.  Adjustments to

and notification to the Contractor.

accordance with the Contractor’s Hazardous and Industrial Materials Cleanup and

and Industrial Material Storage area until proper disposal of such waste is feasible.  
The Contractor may use the subcontracting services of a firm specializing in the 
management and disposal of such materials and waste, if/when directed by the 
Town. 
 
5.10 Stumps:

loaded, transported, stored, reduced, and disposed of in accordance with the

with Stump Conversion Table – Diameter to Volume Capacity.
