6:00 PM 
 
 
BUDGET HEARING & AGENDA HEARING 
 
Note: October 25, 2022 Published Advertisement 
 
Mayor Pro-tem Crenshaw called the meeting to order 6:03.  Town Administrator Milie provided a summary of the 
budget.  He explained some changes in the revenue streams that were temporary ARPA funding.  He explained the T-
SPLOST failed so any infrastructure planned under this approval now will be under SPLOST Funding.  There was a 
new revenue line item for the festivals which includes sponsorships, for $90,000.  It was expected $75,000 will be 
from sponsorship.  A new revenue fund was added for the traffic camera funding.  Depending on the incoming funding 
from the traffic camera there may be a shift in funding to allow hiring another officer.  The funding from the camera 
must be used for Police equipment.  The $100,000 was from budget savings from each department to go to reserve for 
capital purchases later.  Council Member Skipper-Greer asked if the valve project would be internally or contractually 
done.  Public Works Manager Crosby stated it would be a contract due to proprietary nature of the equipment.  Council 
Member Drohan asked about billing changes that were implemented the beginning the year.  These billing changes 
may show $100,000 to $180,000 saved.  Those savings can be put to infrastructure cost because we will not be 
purchasing the higher rate water.  Mayor Pro-tem Crenshaw states we need to see what the numbers are in November 
and December. 
 
Council Member Drohan spoke on the Thunderbolt Development Resource Utilization Project.  This project is to 
inform Council and Planning & Zoning of water and sewer availability and infrastructure needed.  The raw data has 
been compiled and is now being analyzed.   
 
There were no more questions on the budget.  Mayor Pro-tem closed the public hearing 6:32 pm 
 
 
REGULAR MEETING 
 
1. Call to order   
 
Mayor Pro-tem called the regular business meeting to order at 6:32 pm. 
 
2. Roll Call by Presiding Officer with Invocation, followed by the Pledge of Allegiance  
 
Those in attendance were Mayor Pro-tem David Crenshaw and Council Members:  Bethany Skipper-Greer, Larry 
Ward, Ed Drohan, James Lavin and Brooks Barbaree.  Staff in attendance:  Town Administrator, Bob Milie; Town 
Attorney, Charlie Barrow; Clerk of Council, Deatre Denion; Utilities/Accts. Payable Manager, Katina Spaulding; 
Police Chief, Sean Clayton; Fire Chief, Andrew Bateman and Public Works Manager, Oscar Crosby. 
 
Mayor Dana Williams was not in attendance. 
 

 
3. Approval of Meeting Agenda  
 
Mayor Pro-tem Crenshaw asked for a motion to approve adding the proclamation for the Chatham County Retired 
Teachers at the end of the agenda.  Council Member Drohan made the motion to approve the additional 
proclamation with a second from Council Member Ward.  The motion passed unanimously.  Mayor Pro-tem 
Crenshaw asked for a motion to approve the amended agenda.  Council Member Ward made the motion with a 
second from Council Member Drohan.  The motion passed unanimously. 
 
4. Approval of Minutes from Previous Meeting: 
Minutes of October 12, 2022 
 
Mayor Pro-tem Crenshaw asked for a motion to approve the October 12, 2022 meeting minutes.  Council 
Member Drohan made the motion with a second from Council Member Ward.  The motion passed 
unanimously. 
 
5. Presentations: 
 
a) Finance Report: 
 
Shannon Brett, Holland Bromley, Barnhill &Brett, provided a financial update.  Ms. Brett explained she, 
Town Administrator and Council Member Crenshaw were meeting with the Bank to make sure the accounts 
are in the best possible position.  October revenues are 5.6 million which are in line with the time of the year.  
Expenses are at 5.1 million.  The town has used 72% of the budget.  Ms. Brett explained they have begun the 
year end task:  SPLOST ad, fire subscription, immigration, and payroll for year end.  Council Member Drohan 
asked about the revenue reserve for W & S interest.  Town Administrator Milie stated it is very low.  Council 
Member Drohan asked if there is any value in having a rainy-day fund.  Mayor Pro-temp Crenshaw stated 
GMA suggests six months of reserves.  The law requires 30%.  Ms. Brett stated the way it is handled now is 
the reasonable way to do it.  Ward asked if they are shopping around.  Milie reached out to others and they 
were a few he liked but United is in our jurisdiction and would make it easier to do business with them.  
Crenshaw stated we should shop around but leave it with United.   
 
6. Consent Agenda: 
 
Mayor Pro-tem Crenshaw asked to pull a and c to the debate agenda and asked for a motion to approve the 
outdoor event permit for Wesley Oak Methodist Church.  Council Member Ward made motion to approve with 
a second by Council Member Skipper-Greer.  The motion passed unanimously. 
 
a) Approval of the Thunderbolt Museum Lease.   Staff Recommends Approval. 
 
Town Administrator Milie recapped the concern Thunderbolt Museum had with paying the $500.  There was a 
follow up with Mayor Williams and Anna Maria Thomas to discuss options for the museum and the museum 
agrees to the $500 rental cost.  Council Member Drohan made a motion to approve with a second from 
Council Member Ward.  Council Member Drohan asked if the lease has an out for the museum.  Town 
Administrator Milie stated there is an out and so does the Town.  Mayor Pro-tem Crenshaw called for a vote.  
The motion passed unanimously. 
 
b) Formal Approval of the Wesley Oak Methodist Church Outdoor Event held October 29, 2022.  This Event 
Was Approved by Email but it Needs to be Approved Formally by Council. 
 
c) Approval of the Cost Recovery Agreement and Resolution for Fire Department.  This Agreement Allows a 
Billing to Insurance Companies Only and Only for Non-County Residents.  The Billing is handled by a 
Third Party.  This Agreement is Being Used Around the County.  The Resolution Allows for this Billing to 
be Implemented.  Staff Recommends Approval.  
 

 
Town Administrator Milie explained this is for cost recovery for accidents and other events.  This is billed 
to the insurance company.  It will not pertain to town residents.  Only services performed by Thunderbolt 
Fire will be charged.  This company is the same one being used regionally.  This will be a new revenue 
stream.  The company fees are 40% of the bill.  Estimated revenue for a small town about $10,000.  Council 
Member Lavin asked about the billing process.  Town Administrator Milie provided an example.  Council 
Member Lavin wanted to know the liability to the town.  Town Administrator Milie stated there is no 
additional cost.  The Fire Department will need to adjust their current reporting process.  Council Member 
Skipper-Greer asked Fire Chief Bateman if he felt comfortable with the changes.  Chief explained several 
of the part timers are already using this company and are comfortable with the reporting process.  Mayor 
Pro-tem Crenshaw asked about charging by Federal numbers.  Chief Bateman explains it is based on the 
federal average of what is used and time spent.  Council Member Ward asked about double billing.  Chief 
Bateman stated no issue.  Council Member Drohan made the motion to approve with a second from 
Council Member Skipper-Greer.  The motion was unanimously approved. 
 
 
7. Discussion Agenda: 
 
a) Discussion of a One Dollar Increase in Sanitation Cost.   
 
Town Administrator Milie explained the sanitation cost for 2023 was not covered by the monthly fees.  There is 
no reserve in sanitation, so we do not make any money.  Atlantic Waste went up this year and the monthly fee 
stayed the same and was absorbed by the Town. There are two options for the town. Option 1:  raise the 1 dollar 
to pay for the actual cost of sanitation.  Option 2 the Town absorbs the rate increase.  Council Member Ward 
asked if businesses pay more than residential.  Utilities Manager Spaulding explained businesses have an option 
to hire someone or pay the Town.  Council Member Drohan stated the infrastructure cost is built in.  There is no 
inherent cost.  I think there should be a flat rate to pay for itself.  Council Member Skipper-Greer asked it the 
resident or business has more than one can is there an addition cost per can.  Town Administrator Milie 
explained they must pay for additional cans.  He also stated he is working with Atlantic Waste on options to 
reduce recycling pick up to every other week.  Council Member Drohan stated most citizens do not have full 
cans in a week.  Council Member Drohan made the motion to increase the sanitation fee by $1.  Council 
Member Skipper-Greer seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.

Thunderbolt  
Heroic Citizen Recognition 
 
Whereas, our citizens are the backbone of the Town of Thunderbolt, and 
Whereas, we are made proud daily of the achievements of our active and 
engaged citizenry, and 
Whereas, there comes a time when some or one of our residents perform 
an act that is above and beyond any expectation of the public, and 
Whereas, it is incumbent upon the Town of Thunderbolt to extend our 
gratitude and recognition to such individuals. 
Whereas, in this instance Mr. Rufus “Rusty” Hunter was at his home on 
Bonaventure Road, on October 12th, 2022, when he heard tires screeching 
and a loud collision.  
Whereas, upon arrival at the crash site outside his home, Rusty found a 
vehicle wrecked into a tree and on fire. Due to the location of the damage and 
flames, Mr. Hunter removed the victim by dragging across the front seat, and 
out of the passenger door.  Furthermore, Mr. Hunter removed the victim to 
safe location across the street away from traffic and the intense heat of the 
vehicle fire.  
Whereas, Mr. Hunter put himself in great danger to aid and assist an 
unknown citizen, his actions went above and beyond any expectation on the 
public, his efforts are valued, and recognized.  
Now, Therefore, I- Dana Williams, Mayor of the Town of Thunderbolt, proudly 
bring down my hand to seal this award to Mr. Rusty Hunter as an outstanding 
heroic citizen, that we are all better because of…  
 
Mayor Dana Williams

Mayor & Council,  
 
Thunderbolt staff brings before you a recommendation to send two properties to the planning and zoning 
commission to review for redistricting from the Victory Drive District (VDD) to the River Drive District 
(RDD).  
 
Current Boundaries of both districts see in Exhibit A & Exhibit B. 
 
The two properties are referenced in the photos following…  
 
Properties 
 2701 River Drive (30004 08013) – Exhibit C 
 
2703 River Drive (30004 08012) – Exhibit D

zoning laws (pertaining to the VDD zoning) that would allow certain uses excluded elsewhere on River Drive 
such as liquor stores, gas station etc.… and have different size requirements etc.  
 
Staff recommends the approval to move this to the planning and zoning committee to begin the process of public 
involvement etc.

General Fund 
 
 
 
The General Fund is the primary operating budget of the Town.  The General Fund accounts for the financial resources of the Town 
which are not accounted for in any other fund.  Principal revenue sources are property taxes, beverage taxes, state and local use tax, 
licenses, permits and fines.  The General Fund accounts for expenditures related to the general administration of the Town (general 
government), the protection and safety of people within the Town, including police, fire services, code enforcement (public safety), the 
maintenance and upkeep of infrastructure and Town property within the Town (public works), as well as providing a sense of 
community.   
 
This section of the budget is organized as follows: 
 
1. General Fund Summary – shows revenues grouped by source and expenditures by function 
 
2. General Fund Detailed Revenues Budget – provides revenue by line item for each major revenue source 
 
3. General Fund Expenditures by Department – shows the detailed expenditures for each department in the General Fund

Capital Fund 
 
Fund / Department Description: 
 
The majority of the Town’s capital purchases are infrastructure projects, building renovations or larger vehicle and equipment purchases 
that are not SPLOST or Utility fund related.  These non-routine purchases deemed to be one-time expenditures and not SPLOST or 
Utility Fund related are accounted for in the Capital Fund.  These purchases are financed through either current year General Fund 
budget surplus or General Fund unreserved fund balance.   
In previous years, all capital purchases were accounted for in the General Fund operating budget.  To better understand the recurring 
operational costs of the Town, management has separated capital purchases from the general fund.  In order to effectively manage 
the replacement of these items, the condition, estimated useful life, and anticipated replacement dates for each asset will be reviewed 
by department heads each year.  Based on this input, these items are then prioritized and incorporated in the annual capital budget in 
a way that attempts to manage the annual funding levels needed to replace these assets in a fiscally sustainable manner.   
 
Capital Asset Policy:  Capital assets are defined by the Town as assets with an initial cost of more than $1,000 for general capital 
assets and infrastructure assets, and an estimated useful life in excess of two years.  All capital assets are valued at historical cost or 
estimated historical cost if actual amounts are unavailable.

Water / Sewer Utility Fund 
 
Department Description: 
 
The Water / Sewer Utility Fund is considered a proprietary fund.  It accounts for the provision of water and sewer services to the 
residents of the Town.  All activities necessary to provide such services are accounted for in this fund.  Activities include providing high-
quality drinking water, wastewater treatment and disposal services, and adequate water under sufficient pressure to our residents, 
businesses and visitors.   
During fiscal year 2022, the Water / Sewer Utility had a rate study that resulted in adjustments to water / sewer rates.  These 
adjustments are reflected in this budget.

services to the residents of the Town.  The Town contracts out the garbage collections of residential and commercial customers with a 
3rd party to provide solid waste collection services.  All activities necessary to provide such services are accounted for in this fund. The 
Town charges a per cart fee for monthly service.

INTERGOVERNMENTAL AGREEMENT AND CONTRACT 
 
This Intergovernmental Agreement is made and entered into on the day written below on 
said signature page by and between the Town of Thunderbolt, Georgia, (hereinafter referred to as 
“Town”), Chatham County, a political subdivision of the State of Georgia, (hereinafter referred to 
as “County”) and Sonya L. Jackson, Tax Commissioner of Chatham County (hereinafter referred 
to as “Tax Commissioner”) said parties being collectively referred to as “Parties.” 
 
W I T N E S S E T H 
WHEREAS, Town desires to enter into a contract with the Tax Commissioner to oversee 
and provide the collection of tax monies on behalf of the Town; and 
WHEREAS, O.C.G.A. §48-5-359.1(a)(2)(B) provides that the Tax Commissioner, in her 
discretion, may agree to contract with a Town to prepare the tax digest, assessment and collection 
of taxes in the same manner as would be collected from the county; and  
WHEREAS, the County, subject to approval of the undertaking by the Tax Commissioner, 
desires to assist the Town in the collection of taxes to allow for a more efficient tax collection 
process; and 
WHEREAS, entering into this Agreement would benefit the tax payers of the Town and 
would result in less monies being expended by the Town for purposes of tax collections and would 
assist the County in efficiencies of its operation. 
NOW, THEREFORE, the County, Town and the Tax Commissioner enter into this 
Agreement in consideration of the mutual covenants herein set forth and other good and valuable 
consideration as is more particularly agreed as follows: 

 
 
1. 
Upon the millage rate being set each year by the Town, Tax Commissioner shall prepare 
the tax digest for the Town, based upon the millage rate as fixed and determined by the governing 
body of the Town of Thunderbolt, levy, assess and collect all taxes for the Town in the same 
manner as taxes for Chatham County are levied, assessed and collected, apply and invoke any 
remedies, methods and procedures authorized and/or permitted by law for collection of Town 
taxes, account for and remit to Town all taxes collected in a reasonable and timely manner after 
the date of collection, oversee all Town tax collection, billing, delinquent collections and all other 
matters regarding the collection of Town taxes and perform such supervisory duties as required in 
connection with that Agreement of even date herewith between Chatham County and the Town of 
Thunderbolt regarding the collection of Town taxes. 
 
2. 
In consideration of the County’s assistance, and the Tax Commissioner undertaking such 
additional tax collection, supervision, duties and obligations in addition to her duties required by 
law, the Town shall pay the total sum of $4.00 for each such parcel of real estate and/or bill of 
personalty issued by the Tax Commissioner. The County shall receive $1.25 and the Tax 
Commissioner, for her additional duties and services, shall receive $2.75. The Parties agree that 
the amount of such compensation is appropriate for the County and the Tax Commissioner 
accepting additional duties and responsibilities related to the collection of Town taxes.  
 
3. 
The County and/or Tax Commissioner shall prepare and timely submit to the Town the 
following reports: 

(a) 
TO BE FURNISHED ANNUALLY: 
(I) 
Preliminary tax digest totals; 
(ii) 
Exempt list by name; and 
(Iii) 
The County will accept Town of Thunderbolt Homestead applications; 
(b) 
TO BE FURNISHED MONTHLY BREAKDOWN BY CATEGORY OF TAX 
MONEY COLLECTED BY TAX YEAR FOR: 
(I) 
Revenue disbursement by authority report which will contain a listing of the 
funds collected, interest and refunds; and a recap sheet listing the 
breakdown of funds distributed. 
 
4. 
All payments required by the above paragraph two (2) shall be paid by the Town on or 
before January 30, 2023 for the purposes of collecting 2023 Town taxes.  Subsequent payments 
shall be paid on or before September 15, 2023, for purposes of collecting 2024 Town taxes, and 
on said date of each successive year in which this Contract remains in force.   
 
5. 
This Agreement shall commence upon the signatures of all parties and shall terminate when 
the Tax Commissioner’s term expires.  However, if any of the parties wishes to terminate this 
Agreement they must notify the party not less than sixty (60) days prior to December 31, 2023 or 
any subsequent year in which the contract is in effect.  Such written notices shall be mailed, 
Certified Mail, Return Receipt Requested, to the following:

6. 
It is the intention of the Parties that the laws of Georgia shall govern the validity of this 
Agreement, the construction of its terms and the interpretation of the rights or duties of the Parties. 
 
7. 
In the event any provision or portion of this Agreement is held by any court of competent 
jurisdiction to be invalid or unenforceable, such holding shall not affect the remainder hereof and 
the remaining provisions shall continue in full force and effect to the same extent as would have 
been the case had such invalid or unenforceable provision or portion had never been a part hereof. 
 
8. 
On and after the date of this Agreement, all parties shall, at the request of the other, make, 
execute, and deliver or obtain delivery all instruments and documents and shall do or cause to be 
done all such other things which either party may reasonably require to effectuate the provisions 
and intentions of this Agreement. 
 
9. 
Time is and shall be of the essence of this Agreement. 
 
10. 

The Parties signing this Agreement hereby state that they have the authority to bind the 
entity on whose behalf they are signing. 
 
11. 
This Agreement supersedes all prior discussions and agreements between the parties. This 
Agreement contains the sole and entire understanding between the parties with respect to the 
transactions contemplated by this Agreement and all promises, inducements, offers, solicitations, 
agreements, representations and warranties heretofore made between the parties, if any, are merged 
into this Agreement.  This Agreement shall not be modified or amended except by written 
instrument executed by or on behalf of the parties in the same manner in which this Agreement is 
executed. 
 
12. 
This Agreement shall be binding upon the parties hereto unless otherwise terminated in 
accordance with this Agreement. Should the Town elect to terminate this Agreement during a 
calendar year, and if Tax Commissioner has started the Tax/Billing and/or Collection process for 
said calendar year, the Tax Commissioner and the County shall be entitled to receive compensation 
as contemplated for that calendar year with the Town having no additional obligation thereafter.   
 
13. 
Words of any gender used in this Agreement shall be held and construed to include any 
other gender, and words of a singular number shall be held to include the plural, and vice-versa,

to or otherwise mentioned. 
 
15. 
All references to paragraphs or subparagraphs shall be deemed to refer to the appropriate 
paragraphs or subparagraphs of this Agreement.  Unless otherwise specified in this Agreement, 
the terms “herein”, “hereof”, “hereinafter”, “hereunder” and other terms of like or similar import, 
shall be deemed to refer to this Agreement, as a whole, and not to any particular paragraph or 
subparagraph hereof.  
 
16. 
Except as expressly limited by the terms of this Agreement, all rights, powers and 
privileges conferred hereunder shall be cumulative and not restrictive of those provided at law or 
in equity.
