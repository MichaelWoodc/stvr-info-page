a) Water & Sewer Committee – Council Member Drohan 
 
Council Member Drohan gave the following presentation. 
 
Graywater refers to domestic wastewater generated in households or office buildings from streams without fecal 
contamination, i.e., all household effluent except for the wastewater from toilets. Sources of graywater 
include sinks, showers, baths, washing machines or dishwashers.  
As graywater contains fewer pathogens than domestic wastewater, it is generally safer to handle and easier to treat 
and reuse onsite for toilet flushing, landscape or crop irrigation, and other non-potable uses.  
The application of graywater reuse in urban water systems provides substantial benefits for both the water 
supply subsystem, by reducing the demand for fresh clean water, and the wastewater subsystems by reducing the 
amount of wastewater required to be conveyed and treated. Treated greywater has many uses, such as toilet 
flushing or irrigation (Wikipedia). 

 
These systems generally have an inline filter that removes particulate from the effluent. This filtered effluent then 
is then available for passive residential irrigation or adapted to be utilized for flushing toilets. 
 
Graywater Realities in General and in Thunderbolt 
The percentage of water used for tubs and showers makes up about 60% of the total water usage in the average 
home.  Using a conservative 6500 gallons for the average home in Thunderbolt, we come up with the following 
numbers: 
6500×.60 equals 3900 gallons/ month = 46,800 gallons/year per residence. 
If there were but 100 Conservation Citizens in Thunderbolt who participated in a landscape graywater program that 
would equal to 4,680,000 fewer gallons of sewage processed by Savannah per year.  The costs of sewage 
processing we are charged by Savannah equals $.005856 per gallon.  This equals a potential savings of about 
$22.84 per average residential consumer per month against the current fees paid by Thunderbolt to the City of 
Savannah.  Through the participation by 100 Conservation Citizens, that would equal about $27,406 in real savings 
to Thunderbolt every year.  These savings would allow us to provide rebates to individuals interested in graywater 
systems for their homes. These rebates could be up to 100% of the projected savings with no net costs to the Town 
as these Conservation Citizens would still be participating in the system maintenance costs of W&S through the 
difference between their gross sewer charge and the rebate amount. 
Graywater- The Long View 
1. Water conservation is the reality of any successful W&S system. 
2. Funding models in Thunderbolt need to be flexible enough to engage marginal savings as our costs become 
more and more dependent on what the City of Savannah charges us for water and sewer services. 
3. The potential savings from a Graywater option are better than dollar for dollar as residential sewer fess will 
always exceed any rebate amount up to 100% even with an extended horizon. 
4. The more homes that participate, the more the Town saves. 
 
The Graywater Proposal 
1- Allow some conservation idiot the opportunity to create a graywater system in his home utilizing only shower 
and tub effluent with a passive system that percolates into his surrounding yard area for the purpose of 
irrigation of flowers, shrubbery and grass (if any) growing in his yard. This needs to be someone so 
conservation conscious that he would build this system with his own funds. 
2- Use the results of this pilot project to estimate the costs of building these systems for the average residence. 
3- Compare the outcome of this pilot project to the proposal assumptions to provide the statistics to validate the 
proposal and determine its viability. 
4- If viable, utilize this pilot project to model an ordinance allowing for the expansion of residential graywater in 
Thunderbolt.

Mayor Williams read a statement in answering a filing from 43 citizens 
 
“I would like to begin by thanking the Town Administrator, Bob Milie, for his diligent effort in making sure that 
we were able to hold our January Town Council meeting.  Mr. Milie spent the entire day working with IT trying to 
get the remote platform set up as our Town Clerk and Town Receptionist were both our with COVID.  Mr. Milie 
had never conducted a meeting in this manner and in spite of the circumstances was able to make it happen.  In 
addition to two of the three Town Hall employees being absent with COVID, myself and two other members of 
council were also out with it as well.  Despite having a 103 fever due to having the flu and COVID simultaneously, 
I decided not to postpone the meeting because I knew that several vendors and citizens had made plans that needed 
Council approval in order to move on with their projects without suffering setbacks and financial delays.  It wasn’t 
perfect but we made it through. 
 
On Thursday, February 3, 2022, former Mayor Beth Goette delivered a letter that she and several other citizens 
signed calling out several mistakes that I made as my first time as Mayor while doing so remotely and suffering 
from flue and COVID. 
 
The first issue was the PIN to join the meeting was posted incorrectly on the Town website.  The email sent to 
Mayor, Council, Staff and presenters had the correct link and PIN to join the meeting.  When the PIN was added to 
the website a keystroke error caused one number to be off which is why people initially had an issue log in on.  As 
soon as the mistake was discovered it was immediately corrected.  Second it was suggested this was done 
intentionally so Council could vote on Mayor Pro Tempore without asking for public input, which has never been 
done nor is it customary anywhere else to my knowledge.  It was also suggested I made a mistake by moving the 
selection of the Mayor Pro Tempore without asking for a motion and a vote to move an item.  The Town Attorney 
stated a motion and vote were only necessary if adding an item to the agenda.  It was also suggested myself and 
Council needed a refresher course on Town elections and Council Meetings, however as stated in the Town 
Charter:  Section 6.- Town Council; Power; compensation; Mayor Pro Tempore.  As the first regular meeting of 
the town council following a regular election of a mayor and aldermen or as soon thereafter as practicable, the 
council shall select one of its members as mayor pro tempore who shall, in the event of the absence, disability or 
disqualification of the mayor, perform all duties and exercise all rights, powers and privileges of the office of 
mayor. 
 
We followed the Town Charter to the letter.  A Mayor Pro Tempore was selected by Council and agreed upon by a 
majority of 4 to 3.  I was instructed by GMA to always follow the Charter and that is exactly what we did.  The 
suggestion that it was done so nefariously is outright wrong and was not done to hurt or wrong anyone.  We all 
have a great deal of respect for Councilman Lavin.  After the meeting, Councilman Drohan informed me that he 
chose Councilman Crenshaw because of his long service to the Town and his vast knowledge of how the Town 
operates.  He also felt in event the Mayor Pro Tempore had to fulfill his duties as Mayor, Councilman Crenshaw 
would be able to step in and sever the Town without any problems  
 
In response to the suggestion, I had some ulterior motive for moving the selection of Mayor Pro Tempore to the 
beginning of the meeting, it was done simply because I was very sick, we were using a new platform for the 
meeting and the Town Administrator was conducting the hybrid meeting for the first time with no assistance.  I 
wanted to ensure in the event I felt too ill to continue or we lost communication the Mayor Pro Tempore would be 
able to continue the meeting. 
 
In closing I would like to thank everyone again for their patience and understanding for the last month’s meeting.  
Unfortunately, several of use contracted COVID which added to the stress and difficulty of our fist meeting as the 
newly elected Mayor and Council of Thunderbolt.  We made a few mistakes, but given the circumstances, I think 
we did the best we could.  Going forward, we will make every effort to continue to follow the Town Charter, our 
ordinances and the Law.  It is our sworn duty and obligation to represent ALL of the citizens of Thunderbolt 
equally and to the best of our ability and that is exactly what we will do.

PROCLAMATION of APPRICATION  
And 
RECOGNITION 
 
 
WHEREAS, Fred B. Corey was a long-standing member of the Town of 
Thunderbolt as a resident; and 
 
 
WHEREAS, Mr. Corey not only was a passionate advocate for the Town as a 
resident, he concurrently dedicated the majority of his adult working life to Thunderbolt; 
and 
 
 
WHEREAS, Mr. Corey began his career with the Thunderbolt Department of 
Public works in 1969 and retired after thirty-seven years of service in 2006; and 
 
 
WHEREAS, Mr. Corey started as a sanitation employee and served in all 
capacities of the department until his retirement as the Public Works Director; and 
 
 
WHEREAS, it is recognized that Mr. Corey’s steadfast dedication to the 
residents, businesses, and visitors was evident though his work ethic, responsiveness to 
the needs of the community, and mentorship to other employees; and 
 
 
WHEREAS, the Town of Thunderbolt – Mayor and Council value that the lives 
of the members of the public were enhanced by Mr. Corey’s efforts to ensure that he truly 
did “leave it better than he found it”; 
 
 
NOW, THEREFORE, I, Dana Williams, Mayor of Thunderbolt, on behalf of the 
Town Council, citizens, businesses and employees of the Town of Thunderbolt, take 
great pleasure in presenting this Proclamation of Appreciation and Recognition to the 
surviving family of Mr. Fred B. Corey. 
 
 
MOREOVER, may it be accepted that for the next thirty-seven years, March 28th 
will be recognized as Fred B. Corey Day in the Town of Thunderbolt, a day to reflect on 
the accomplishments and positive impact Mr. Corey had on the Town and all her 
citizenry.  
 
 
IN WITNESS THEREOF, I do hereby set my hand and cause the seal of the 
Town of Thunderbolt to be affixed this 9th day of March, 2022.

Thunderbolt Police Department 
Letter of Recognition 
Presented To: 
Charles Wilson “CJ” Badge # 511 
Recognition for the  
Capture of an Armed Robbery Suspect 
 
On February 23, 2022 at approximately 2145 hours, Patrolman Wilson was 
dispatched to an armed robbery at the Parker’s Convenience Store located at 3105 
E. Victory Drive.  The suspect ran from the store and Patrolman Wilson called for 
assistance from Savannah Police Department to setup a perimeter since he was 
alone and actively searching for an armed subject. 
With the intent to apprehend an alleged dangerous felon from the streets, 
Patrolman Wilson continued pursuit while officers from Savannah Police 
Department cordoned of a perimeter. Patrolman Wilson noticed the suspect 
crouching and hiding in a dark alleyway near the Thunderbolt Fire Department.  
Making a conscious decision to ensure the safety of the public over himself, 
Patrolman Wilson exited his vehicle and attempted to apprehend the suspect.  The 
subject fled and a foot pursuit began, with knowledge of the perimeter, Patrolman 
Wilson expertly chased and pushed the suspect closer to the SPD officers. 
Eventually, Patrolman Wilson caught and apprehend the suspect with assistance 
from SPD.  
Not only was a person of interest caught while eluding arrest, the money and 
weapon were also recovered in the possession of the individual.  This arrest brings 
an individual closer to justice for the robbery, but it is believed that it may also 
assist in the case closures of other armed robberies in the Town of Thunderbolt, 
and City of Savannah.  
Your brave and selfless actions that evening bring great credit to the Town of 
Thunderbolt, Thunderbolt Police Department, law enforcement community, and 
certainly you as a dedicated and consummate professional.   Thunderbolt Mayor 
and Council, Town staff, and Thunderbolt residents and guests are safer today 

because of you, your actions, and dedication to protect those you have sworn to 
serve.  
Be it recorded in the official minutes of the Town of Thunderbolt – Town Council 
meeting on March 9th, 2022 that we owe you and all those like you our deepest 
respect and gratitude.  Job well done.

Agreement for Event Planning Services Blessing of the Fleet 
& Social Media Management Services 
Town of Thunderbolt, Georgia 
 
This agreement is made and entered into by and between The Town of Thunderbolt, whose 
address is 2821 River Drive, Thunderbolt, GA 31404 (“Client“), and Simply Savannah Marketing 
(“Simply Savannah Marketing”), whose address is 213 E Hall St., Savannah, GA 31401. 
 
1. Services.  
a. Simply Savannah Marketing will provide the Client with event planning services 
for The Town of Thunderbolt Blessing of the Fleet on Saturday, May 28, 2022, 
along River Drive. The client agrees to hire Simply Savannah Marketing as an 
independent contractor for the sole purpose of advising and consulting for the 
planning and execution of this event. At all times during the course of this 
contract, the Client retains all decision-making power and responsibility for the 
final execution of the event. 
b. Simply Savannah Marketing will provide social media management for a period 
of six (6) months beginning March 1, 2022, to August 31, 2022, for the Town of 
Thunderbolt.  
 
2. Payment terms and conditions. All fees are quoted in and shall be paid in USD (United 
States dollars). Invoices must be made via QuickBooks or by check to Simply Savannah 
Marketing. All fees are nonrefundable. 
 
3. Payment schedule 
a. Event Planning Services Blessing of the Fleet and Social Media Management for 
the Town of Thunderbolt ($26,000) 
i. 
Prior to or upon signing this contract, the Client agrees to pay Simply 
Savannah Marketing $6,500. This deposit constitutes 25 percent (25%) of 
the total event organizer service fees of $26,000 as recited above. 
ii. 
Before May 28, 2022 or one day before the Blessing of the Feet Event if it 
is postponed, whichever comes later, the Client agrees to pay Simply 
Savannah Marketing the remaining $19,500 and any and all additional 
approved project expenses. There is a $100 late fee per diem for every 
24-hour period that this payment is past due. 
iii. 
Kill fee. Should the parties (the Client and Simply Savannah Marketing) 
fail to secure $35,000 in corporate sponsorship funds by March 25, 2022, 
then the Client  may cancel the contract and the Simply Savannah 
Marketing will retain the $6,500 deposit and will continue Social Media 
Services until July 15th, 2022.or the client may reschedule the event to 
September or October and efforts to secure corporate of other 
sponsorships will continue.  
iv. 
It is recognized that the entire festival expenses are anticipated to be 
approximately $70,000, and Simply Savannah Marketing is expected via 

sponsorship to cover all costs of said festival. The expected expenses in 
addition to the $26,000 to be paid to Simply Savannah Marketing will 
include but not be limited to Stage Rental, Live Radio Broadcast, Sound 
System, Trolly Rental, Event Signage & Flyers, Special Event Permit 
Application. Portable Toilets, Medical Tent, Bishop Fee, Social Media 
Paid Promotion, Digital TV/Radio Advertising, event insurance and Town 
costs for overtime expenses of employees before, during and after the 
event.   Any money secured over the amount of festival related costs born 
by the Town of Thunderbolt, will remain with the Town of Thunderbolt to 
be used for public good (park upgrades, other events for residents and 
the like.  
 
4. Event vendors. The Client agrees to hire event vendors that are licensed in their fields and 
who are insured. 
 
5. Contract term. Both parties agree that the term of this Agreement shall begin when the 
Client’s deposit has cleared through the bank and shall end when the event services are 
complete. 
 
6. Liability. The Client assumes all liability for the event and is responsible for obtaining event 
insurance coverage at the Client’s discretion.

SERVICE AGREEMENT 
 
 
THIS AGREEMENT made and entered into this day of __________, 2022, (this “Agreement”) 
between the MAYOR AND ALDERMEN OF THE CITY OF SAVANNAH, a municipal 
corporation organized and existing under the laws of the State of Georgia (herein after referred 
to as “Savannah”), and the TOWN OF THUNDERBOLT GEORGIA, a municipal corporation 
organized and existing under the laws of the State of Georgia (herein after referred to as 
“Thunderbolt"): 
 
WHEREAS, sewage collected by the sewage system owned and operated by Thunderbolt is 
treated at the President Street sewage treatment facility owned and operated by Savannah; and 
 
WHEREAS, the President Street Treatment Facility is operated by Savannah under a National 
Pollution Discharge Elimination System (“NPDES”) permit issued by the Georgia 
Environmental Protection Division ("EPD"); and 
 
WHEREAS, Thunderbolt has selected sewage treatment under which Thunderbolt will deliver 
sewage to the Savannah Treatment Facility, by a constructed sewage transport system. 
 
NOW THEREFORE, in consideration of the mutual covenants and agreements, and benefits to 
the parties, Savannah and Thunderbolt agree as follows: 
 
All sewage delivered by Thunderbolt to Savannah shall be normal strength domestic sewage and 
have originated within the town limits of Thunderbolt. 
 
For purposes of this agreement, the term normal strength domestic sanitary sewage shall mean 
sewage meeting the requirements of Section 9 of this Agreement and having an annual average 
five-day biological oxygen demand ("BOD") of no more than 193 mg/L. 
 
Savannah, in its sole discretion, may install and fund any telemetry or SCADA monitoring 
equipment desired to monitor and measure the delivery of sewage.  Savannah currently 
maintains a Mission Realtime Monitoring system to which the flow meter sends data. 
 
After installation and inspection, Savannah shall assume ownership and maintenance 
responsibility for the Sewage Meter. Savannah shall be given unfettered access to the Sewage 
Meter for maintenance, reading, and calibration purposes. 
 
1. Subject to Audit. Savannah and Thunderbolt shall cooperate in any audit and make available 
records concerning building permits and connections to its sewer system to permit 
verification of compliance with this section. 
  
2.  System Maps and Plans. Thunderbolt shall, upon reasonable request, provide Savannah with 

updated diagrams or maps showing the sewer lines and lots in areas served by the Thunderbolt 
sewer system, and master plans for areas of future planned sewer service. Such updated diagrams, 
maps or master plans shall be provided no less frequently than annually and shall reflect all 
modifications 
 
3.  Thunderbolt Sewage Characteristics, Industrial Pretreatment and Sewer Use Regulations. 
Thunderbolt shall adopt an ordinance which is no less stringent than the sewer use provisions of 
the "Combined Sewer Use and Pretreatment Ordinance" of the City of Savannah as amended 
from time to time, as adopted pursuant to, and in accordance with, the regulations of the EPD or 
any successor agency. This ordinance, inter alia, is intended to prohibit the discharge into the 
President Street sewer system of any materials which by reason of character or quantity would: 
 
 a. Interfere with the operation or efficiency of the sewage transport and treatment systems    
of Savannah, or not be susceptible to treatment by the treatment process. 
 
 b. In any way cause a violation of any directive, regulation, or permit of the EPD or the 
industrial pretreatment program of the City of Savannah or any other regulatory agency. 
 
 c. Constitute a hazard to human or animal life due to toxicity, flammable or explosive 
characteristics of the pollutant(s). 
 
    Thunderbolt shall provide a copy of its sewer use ordinance to Savannah and any 
amendments thereto. Thunderbolt shall notify Savannah of any violation of the sewer use 
ordinance within 24 hours of becoming aware of the violation and take corrective action to stop 
such violation and minimize the effects of such violation.  Thunderbolt shall submit to Savannah 
a revised industrial user inventory on at least an annual basis. 
 
    Thunderbolt shall duly appoint Savannah, by ordinance, as its compliance agent for the 
sanitary sewer use regulations, and if so appointed, Savannah shall enforce the sewer use 
regulations and issue industrial pretreatment permits and grease interceptor waste generator and 
transporter permits within the Town of Thunderbolt. As appointed, Savannah shall provide 
copies to Thunderbolt of any industrial pretreatment permit or grease interceptor waste generator 
or transporter permits issued within Thunderbolt, and copies of all documents related to 
compliance with the sewer use regulations.  Pretreatment implementation and oversight 
activities, including inspection, sampling, and enforcement, shall be conducted by Savannah.  
Access to the facilities of industrial users located within Thunderbolt’s jurisdictional boundaries 
for the purpose of inspection, sampling, and any other duties deemed necessary shall be provided 
to Savannah’s pretreatment program administrator upon request. 
 
    The sewage delivered by Thunderbolt to the Savannah collection system shall contain no 
toxic materials which would interfere with Savannah's biological treatment processes, and shall 
have characteristics which do not exceed the following parameter limits:

municipality's own boundary.

system.

Revize Design Principles  
The Revize Web Application Developers are not only responsible for the look, functionality, and 
performance of your website. They are also responsible for the security of the web content and 
web-based applications they create. They ensure that the code supports secure authentication and 
authorization, and provides access control mechanisms as required. 
 
Good design principles are always based on readability, taking into consideration appropriate font 
type and size for headlines and text area, as well as line height – ensuring all page elements are 
balanced. Our designers also pay meticulous attention to their use of shadows and gradients. To the 
layman’s eye there may not appear to be a shadow, however on the website the font will appear 
sharper (or maybe softer depending on the amount of shadow used).  
 
Of course color cannot be overlooked. Our designers first take the client’s preferences, official logo 
colors, and pictures into consideration to create a color scheme consisting of no more than three 
colors. We then use variants and hues to create visual appeal, contrast, eye-catching allure and 
invoke the overall feeling that the client desires. 
 
Last but not least is effective use of page elements such as call to action buttons, social sharing 
icons, email newsletter sign-up, and promotion areas. The ultimate goal is to provide an easy to 
navigate webpage that is informative without being overwhelming. Therefore, it is the designer’s job 
to guide the client in making appropriate placement choices for needed items. 
Revize Design Trends 
There are some exciting new design trends, and Revize is always on the cutting edge, implementing 
the best of these innovations in our websites. We are especially pleased at how effectively they are 
proving to be in increasing engagement in government websites.  
 
#1 Responsive Web Design – The most important development in website design in years, 
Responsive Web Design (RWD) automatically conforms and optimizes websites for any screen size. 
With the substantial increase in smartphone and tablet users today, people are going online using a 
vast number of devices with wildly different screen sizes. Our websites offer this very important 
feature of easily and cleanly conforming to computer, tablet and mobile device screens. 
 
#2 Liquified Content – This is another important trend that address the fact that information is no 
longer static or concrete. Instead, content is specifically customized for each unique user. Liquidity 
of content enhances the immediacy and flexibility of content. The more liquid your community’s 
content, the easier it is for residents and businesses to access this information in ways and via the 
channels of their choice: fixed or mobile, interactive and live. Revize is able to effectively make your 
content liquid.  This will make it adaptable to various situations and, therefore, easy to reuse in 
different contexts distributed for a variety of display formats and communication channels. 
 
#3 Image Tiles – This is a trend that enables developers to display content in a pin board style of 
display. Revize now offers this feature, which creates a very visually appealing display of content,

Revize Cloud Hosting, Disaster Recovery & Security 
Resilient Hosting: 
Revize uses Amazon Web Services (AWS) Hosting Infrastructure that is offered in multiple Regions 
in the United States and around the World. Each Region is a separate geographic area completely 
isolated one from another. AWS Regions are connected to multiple Internet Service Providers (ISPs) 
and to a private global network backbone to offer lower cost and more consistent cross-region 
network latency when compared with the public internet 
 
A region has multiple, isolated locations known as Availability Zones (AZ). Availability Zones are 
inter-connected through low-latency links. A Virtual Private Center (VPC) spans across multiple 
availability zones.  
 
Revize Web Sites are hosted on a Virtual Machine running Windows Server operating system with 
Internet Information Service (IIS) as a Web Server. Virtual Machine is an EC2 instance in AWS terms 
is hosted inside a VPC in an availability zone of a pre-selected region. A number of EC2 are 
provisioned in multiple locations across the United States inside a Virtual Private Center isolated 
from the Internet and the rest of Amazon Web Services infrastructure to offer faster access to the 
end-users and to minimize the impact of an outage, whether of a specific EC2 instance, an 
availability zone or an entire AWS region.  
 
Disaster Recovery: 
Windows Web Server virtual disk or EC2 Elastic Block Storage (EBS) is backed up on evry night at 
10 PM EST. Additionally, the snapshots or virtual disk backups are subsequently automatically 
copied between regions to enable cross-region recovery capabilities. 
 

 
 
Thunderbolt, Georgia 
 
23 of 44 
 
Should a Web Site hosted by Revize and monitored in automated fashion become unavailable, 
another EC2 instance can be restarted. When that proves insufficient EC2 instance will be 
reprovisioned in the same availability zone, a different availability zone, or, in a different region 
using the latest snapshot in the matter of a couple of minutes. 
 
The incoming request, from the Internet traffic, is bound to a static IP address or Elastic IP in AWS 
terms that leverages NAT to forward traffic to a running EC2 instance private IP address. In the case 
of an EC2 instance re-provisioned in the same region, whether in the same or a different availability 
zone, Elastic IP is re-assigned to the new EC2 instance. 
 
Elastic IP is represented to the public internet using CName or A-Host domain name services entry. 
In case of an EC2 instance or an availability zone failure, no adjustment to domain name service is 
required. In the case of a regional, wide-spread AWS outage, an EC2 instance is reprovisioned in a 
different region, re-using the latest snapshot preserving the content as of the last automated 
backup. However, an Elastic IP is specific to the region and, therefore a change to CName or A-Host 
configuration is required to point to the disaster recovery regional Elastic IP. Revize Disaster 
Recovery Infrastructure Diagram. AZ stands for Availability Zones

Shallow Well Options 
 
Utilizing shallow wells to water residential landscape 
areas has been long discussed as a central part of 
water management in the Town of Thunderbolt. The 
recent challenge regarding this strategy has been the 
significant increases in costs to have these wells 
drilled commercially.  
In my own situation, I ultimately failed to complete 
my well by hand due to the density and depth of the 
dense gray clay substrata directly above the 
subsurface water that often runs 6 to 10 feet deep, 
depending upon your location. At that time, I did turn 
to a commercial well digging company to complete 
the project. The price of that time was reasonable 
and given my strong emphasis on conservation 
practices, I saw this as a reasonable and conservation 
friendly decision on my part.  
 
 

Current Cost Challenges 
 
Recent reports regarding citizens desiring shallow 
wells have been in the $4000 range. By all estimates, 
these current costs will easily surpass whatever 
savings one might gain from reduced possible water 
usage for landscape purposes, even if one were to 
project the savings over a 10-year horizon. As a result, 
a commercial model for the creation of shallow wells 
locally becomes problematic. 
 
Past Practices 
In the past, to minimize the costs associated with 
these projects, there have been groups of three or 
four neighbors who have cooperatively drilled wells 
by hand and as a result, came up with a very cost-
efficient and conservation friendly solution to 
providing water for our yards and gardens. 
 
 
 

Citizens’ Conservation Committee 
 
One of my thoughts has been the creation of a 
Citizens’ Conservation Committee that would be 
made up of interested residents of the Town of 
Thunderbolt who, in conjunction with the ad hoc 
Committee on Water and Sewer, provide feedback to 
the Council regarding the attitudes of the citizens of 
Thunderbolt relative to water conservation practices. 
This committee could also be a mechanism whereby 
citizens could work as a team to create shallow wells 
for one another.  
This could as well be a mechanism for citizens 
interested in a shallow well for their property, who 
because of financial concerns or physical capacity 
cannot create a shallow well on their properties by 
themselves to benefit from the community spirit 
inherent in this type of organization. 
 
 

“To Be or Not To Be” 
 
During the last election cycle, there was a great deal 
of discussion with regards to creating a greater sense 
of community and what has become a rapidly 
changing social and physical landscape in the Town 
of Thunderbolt.  
I consider this an opportunity for the citizens of 
Thunderbolt, with the support of the Town Council 
and the town administration to advance the concepts 
of “neighborliness” and “helping out the other guy” 
as a part of the experience of being a resident and 
citizen and the Town of Thunderbolt.

28. Beware of Cyber Fraud: Fraudulent e-mails attempting to get you to wire money to criminal computer hackers are increasingly common 
in real estate transactions. Under this scam, computer hackers fraudulently assume the online identity of the actual mortgage lender, 
closing attorney and/or real estate broker with whom you are working in the real estate transaction. Posing as a legitimate company, they 
then direct you to wire money to them. In many cases, the fraudulent e-mail is sent from what appears to be the authentic web page of 
the legitimate company responsible for sending the wiring instructions. You should use great caution in sending or receiving funds based 
solely on wiring instructions sent to you by e-mail. Independently verifying the wiring instructions with someone from the company 
sending them is the best way to prevent fraud. In particular, you should treat as highly suspect any follow up e-mails you receive from a 
mortgage lender, closing attorney and/or real estate broker directing you to wire funds to a revised account number. Never verify wiring 
instructions by calling a telephone number provided along with a second set of wiring instructions since you may end up receiving a 
fraudulent verification from the computer hackers trying to steal your money. Independently look up the telephone number of the 
company who is supposed to be sending you the wiring instructions to make sure you have the right one.
