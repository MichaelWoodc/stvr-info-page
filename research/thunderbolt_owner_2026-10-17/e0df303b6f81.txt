part of the world where it seems like everyone wants to live.

Thunderbolt and necessarily drive the choices that we will need to make as a community in both the near and

SUMMARY 
1- The Population of Thunderbolt is relatively steady over the past 15 years and has become notably older over 
the past decade. 
2- The number of households are relatively even over the past decade and the household size has dropped by 
about 20%. 
3- Currently, the residences in Thunderbolt are fairly evenly split between single units and condominiums and 
apartments. 
Property values are about 20% higher than the Savannah area and 25% higher than the State of Georgia. 
4- The educational attainment of the citizens of Thunderbolt is about 25% higher than the Savannah area and 
the State of Georgia. 
Per capita income is about 20% higher than the Savannah area and the State of Georgia. 
Thunderbolt continues to be a consistently diversified community over the past 10 years. 
8

About 60% of the population self-describe as White Collar/Professional. 
In other words, our citizens are a diversified group of older, generally better educated individuals living in 
smaller family units. 
More of our citizens self-identify as white-collar and professional. 
The relatively higher property values likely reflect the desirability of living in Thunderbolt suggesting future 
pressures on the development of available properties that exist here. 
In addition to these data, many people have commented on how Thunderbolt is becoming a prime area for 
first-time homebuyers, bringing in younger couples and growing families. 
All of these factors impact the evolving “Face of Thunderbolt” and as such directly impact the decisions that 
we need to make as a town for our mutual long-term welfare. 
The recent requests for variances on property development have highlighted a notable problem with regards to the 
upper limits of sewer resources allowed us by our contract with the City of Savannah and the clear messages from 
them that those limits are unlikely to be increased. Though these issues have been focused upon the 7-acre tract along 
Bonaventure Avenue, I believe that this is in fact a town wide issue and deserves to be addressed by then Council in 
the near term. 
In pursuit of that goal, I have asked our folks in administration to create a listing of undeveloped/underdeveloped 
properties in Thunderbolt so that we can then approach the likely future demand of development in Thunderbolt 
relative to the realities of our limited capacities in a manner that is responsible to our citizens and equally respects the 
rights of all property owners in Thunderbolt. 
To that end, I have formulated some “Development Zones” that I think reasonably identifies the category and location 
of properties whose development will likely place demands on our infrastructure. Furthermore, I have taken a stab at 
identifying “Development Factors” that should reasonably be assessed for each of these categories. 
The goal of course would be to operationalize a clear understanding of the nature and degree of future development 
demands and to assess those alongside the realities of our limited infrastructure capacities. 
Please review the following information and feel free to help me create a more thorough list of Development Zones 
and Development Factors as well as recommending additional factors relative to this issue that you believe are 
important.

Recognition of Life Saving Awards  
 
The Town of Thunderbolt believes in recognition of our employees, their actions, and outstanding 
efforts to their community, concurrently, we desire to recognize those in and around the community 
when their actions go above and beyond. In keeping with this belief, the Town of Thunderbolt Mayor, 
Council and Thunderbolt Police and Fire Departments recognize the following individuals for their 
outstanding efforts on August 19th, 2022.  
Fire Fighter Kristopher Fain – Meritorious Life Saving Award 
Paramedic Heather Fain – Outstanding Citizen Life Saving Award 
Police Chief Sean Clayton – Letter of Commendation  
Police Lt. Brandon Runyon – Letter of Commendation 
Fire Chief Andrew Bateman – Letter of Commendation 
Fire Fighter Drew Davies – Letter of Commendation 
Fire Fighter Frank Koualczuk – Letter of Commendation 
While attending the Savannah Bananas baseball game at Grayson Stadium for a Thunderbolt employee 
appreciation event. Thunderbolt off-duty police, fire fighters, and their families answered two medical 
needs that arose.  Their actions performed seamlessly and professionally resulted in performing life-
saving CPR on an active player in cardiac arrest on the field, as well as revival and medical care for a 
spectator that had passed out.  
These individuals without hesitation jumped the fence to the field, performed hands on patient 
assessment, ensured access to perform CPR and apply the AED to deliver shocks, when necessary, to the 
patient and restart his heart.  Concurrently, police ensured the scene was secure and access available for 
EMS.  Immediate medical intervention coupled with use of the AED (Automated External Defibrillator) 
ensured that not only was the player saved, he was able to walk off the field under his own strength.  
These actions are the backbone of what we strive for in the Town of Thunderbolt, providing assistance, 
getting involved, and ultimately helping the people you have sworn to protect.  Your actions reflect 
great credit upon yourself, Thunderbolt Police & Fire Department, and the Town of Thunderbolt.  
Therefore, it is my honor as Mayor of the Town of Thunderbolt to bring down my hand and seal these 
Unit Citations and Individual Commendations of Life Saving Award Letters to express my and the Town’s 
appreciation for all of your efforts.

- Discussion of Purpose of TDRUP 
The purpose of this project is to evaluate the 
various challenges related to infrastructure 
relative to potential future development 
within the Town of Thunderbolt. The 
primary goal is to be able to provide to the 
Town Council the pertinent data required in 
order to formulate policy that responsibly 
manages resources, infrastructure and 
development throughout the town 
respecting the rights of property owners and 
the general citizenry relative to fairness and 
quality of life as these might be impacted by 
future development.

Scoring- An Example 
A central issue in this research relates to the 
sewer resources and how it can fairly be 
shared among the stakeholders, citizens and 
developers. I suggest that we use a fairly 
simple analytical model for this purpose. 
 
Daily Limits  
minus Buffer for Emergencies =  
Rational Daily Sewer Resources  
 
Rational Daily Sewer Resources  
minus Current Average Sewer Utilization = 
Available Surplus Daily Sewer Resources 
 
 
110

Each property is then scored based on its 
current zoning status for the number of 
housing units based an assumption of 2.75 
occupants per unit. 
These scored values will then be totaled 
across each District and then Town-wide to 
provide us with the Potential Sewer 
Resource Utilization. 
Available Surplus Daily Sewer Resources 
minus  
Potential Sewer Resource Utilization = 
Projected Sewer Resource Availability. 
 
 
111

Projected Sewer Resource Availability 
provides us with an important but only one 
of the necessary measures Council will need 
to make create responsible, data driven 
policy relative to future development in 
Thunderbolt.
