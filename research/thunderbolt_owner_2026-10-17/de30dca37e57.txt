Thunderbolt Police Department 
Letter of Recognition 
Presented To: 
Corporal Michael Foran Badge # 508 
Recognition for  
Customer Service Excellence  
 
On March 30th, 2023 at approximately midnight, Corporal Foran was dispatched to 
a residence near Oakwood and Pierce Boulevard for the report of a suspicious 
person possibly prowling and knocking on doors. The subject seemed disoriented, 
without a shoe, and requesting a phone to use.  Upon arrival at the scene, Corporal 
Foran immediately made contact with the person in question, ensuring officer and 
citizen safety while sizing up for any threats might have been present. Corporal 
Foran used simple policing techniques that may sometimes be lost.  He fully 
listened, devised a course of action, and proceeded.  
It was Corporal Foran’s gut instinct that the gentleman in distress posed no threat 
and his intervention would be to provide any assistance possible.  Corporal Foran 
ascertained that the individual was chased by a dog while returning to Savannah 
State. In the chase he lost his shoe and phone. This individual while being lost and 
disoriented did what came natural. He sought assistance or help from someone.   
Corporal Foran made contact with the caller. He advised them they did the right 
thing by calling the police and there is no need to answer the door anytime if you 
feel uncomfortable. He also assured them that this was a young man who was just 
needing some help and posed no threat.  
Corporal Foran then provided a ride to where the gentleman remembers losing his 
shoe, and was able to retrieve it from the yard where he jumped a fence to get to 
safety.  Corporal Foran then went with the gentleman to the location he remembers 
losing his phone and was able to locate it, but it had been runover and rendered 
inoperable.   
Corporal Foran then offered his phone to the gentleman to call his mom. The 
individual informed his mom that he was with police. His mother’s immediate 
6

response was “Are you being detained, or are you under arrest?”.  He replied “No 
mom, he is helping me”.  
After retrieving the phone, Corporal Foran was able to give the individual a ride to 
the University and ensured the gentleman’s safe return, with a few parting words.  
“Remember to call your mom, and let her know you got home safe”. 
Daily this Mayor & Council is made proud by the strong and dedicated work by all 
staff members, but in this instance, Corporal Foran, our most senior officer in law 
enforcement showed true Thunderbolt professionalism.  
Corporal Foran’s actions of quickly establishing the possible threat or lack thereof, 
then ensuring all involved individuals were made aware there was no issue, and 
finally to guarantee over and above the respect and service-oriented approach at 
ensuring any individual in our Town was heard, understood, and cared for.  
Corporal Foran your calm and professional demeanor coupled with your approach 
at ensuring all individuals are treated fairly and compassionately bring great credit 
to the Town of Thunderbolt, Thunderbolt Police Department, law enforcement 
community, and certainly you as a dedicated and consummate professional.   
Thunderbolt Mayor and Council, Town staff, and Thunderbolt residents and guests 
are safer today because of you, your actions, and dedication to protect those you 
have sworn to serve.  
Be it recorded in the official minutes of the Town of Thunderbolt – Town Council 
meeting on April 12th, 2023 that we owe you and all those like you our deepest 
respect and gratitude.  Job well done.

BY THE MAYOR OF THE TOWN OF THUNDERBOLT 
A PROCLAMATION 
IN RECOGNITION of “THE WEEPING TIMES” 
WHEREAS, On March 2, 1859, Pierce and John Butler claimed a fortune of approximately 
$700,000, which would have been roughly $20 million in modern times. The Butler Brothers 
would go on to coordinate one of the most somber moments in American history, and  
WHEREAS, On a rainy day at the Ten Broeck Race Course, located on the outskirts of Savannah, 
436 slaves would be brought to the horse stables and sold over a two day period, and  
WHEREAS, This would be labeled as the great slave auction by the news aat the time, an “The 
Weeping Times” by those of African American decent, as well as those who were enslaved at the 
time, and  
WHEREAS, This would be considered “The Weeping Times” from the slaves who were to be sold 
that also corresponded with the rain that lasted from the moment the slaves were brought in until 
the last was sold, as if “the Lord” was crying himself, and 
WHEREAS, This proclamation is made in the memory of those slaves who were sold, and as a 
reminder that we must all remember out history and commit ourselves to progress to ensure that 
no such tragedy happens again. 
NOW THEREFORE,  I DANA WILLIAMS, Mayor of the Town of Thunderbolt, do herby proclaim 
March 2nd-3rd, 2023 as The Weeping Times Remembrance in Georgia.  
In witness thereof, I have hereunto set my hand and caused the Seal of the Executive Department 
to be officiated thus 12th of April in the year 2023.  
 
 Given under my hand 
This 12th day of April 2023

Proclamation 
IN RECOGNITION of “THE BLESSING OF THE FLEET FESTIVAL” 
 
WHEREAS, The Blessing of the Fleet Festival is a celebration of Thunderbolt’s 
heritage and history as a coastal community that allowed residents to sustain an 
excellent quality of life in a beautiful community via fishing and shrimping from the 
vast and rich surrounding waters; and 
 
WHEREAS, the festival has been a storied and recognized tradition that has long since 
been celebrated, the Thunderbolt Mayor and Council have chosen a path to bring about 
a stronger sense of community that acknowledges our past whilst looking to the future, 
and endearing a greater feeling of Town pride; and  
 
WHEREAS, the festival will be highlighted by local craft vendors, regional and ethnic 
food vendors, an entertainment stage, a parade, and ultimately a Blessing for the Fleet; 
and  
 
WHEREAS, this year’s celebration is continuing a renewed and vibrant sense of 
community; and 
 
WHEREAS, as we continue to look forward, we realize we stand upon the shoulders of 
those that came before us, and that we must look back and celebrate with those that paved 
the way for our Town, and for them to be a part of our legacy past, present, and future; 
 
THEREFORE, we recognize the following individuals for their steadfast support, 
leadership, and guiding principles – Mayor Anna Maria Thomas, Councilman John 
Henry, Councilman John Hall, Fire Fighter Bob Morgan, and Franklin Stevens. These 
individuals will be recognized as Fleet Ambassadors for the 2023 Blessing of the Fleet 
Festival, and will serve to continue to share the goodwill and blessings of all the Town of 
Thunderbolt offers; 
 
NOW, THEREFORE, we, The Town of Thunderbolt Town Council and Mayor Dana 
Williams, do hereby proclaim April, 29th, 2023, as “THE BLESSING OF THE FLEET 
FESTIVAL DAY” in Thunderbolt, Georgia, and urge all citizens to participate in this 
cultural event. 
 
IN WITNESS WHEREOF, I Mayor Dana Williams have hereunto set my hand and 
caused the seal of Thunderbolt, Georgia, to be affixed this 12th day of April, 2023.

violation pursuant to Thunderbolt Town laws

As always alcohol is to be consumed responsibly and only by adults twenty-one (21) years of age and

All laws in and out of the Festival grounds will be enforced regarding public drunkenness and 
intoxication – again alcohol is to be consumed in a safe and responsible manner  
- 
DUI enforcement – Police will have a greater presence and maintain a hyper vigilance to ensure our 
Town and residents are protected from impaired drivers

WHY CONSERVATION?
A review of the data indicates that while we are anticipating a 
reduction in the withdrawal allowances from the Floridian Aquifer, the 
Town of Thunderbolt has actually maintained a withdrawal rate well 
within the current and projected allowance. 
One might argue, if that's the case then why should we conserve . The 
answer is simple, it makes good financial sense. We need to have a 
system that allows us to maximize our use of available groundwater 
while constraining the overall costs to our customers. 
I think that we can all agree that the efficient provision of services to 
our citizens is the main goal of effective government. Our failure to 
heed the opportunities to contain costs reflects a failure on the part of 
the Town Council to pursue for this goal for the Citizens of 
Thunderbolt. 
52

Costs and Savings
Costs
• In the past 8 years, Thunderbolt has achieved a significant 
improvement in the infrastructure and provision of water and sewer 
services. I would argue that these improvements have led to a 
significant reduction in wasted/lost water from likely significant 
leaks in those lines that have been replaced. In turn, this has helped 
to keep us within the constraints of the withdrawal limits allowing 
for reduced costs for water services. 
• The reality is that we can anticipate increased costs in terms of 
purchased water from Savannah as well as those fees paid to 
Savannah for sewerage processing. We are a captive audience 
relative to those costs and as such have one option for controlling 
costs and that is to reduce relative consumption. 
53

Savings
• Having the appropriate systems in place to maximize the withdrawal of 
groundwater will necessarily lead to savings within the system as the 
costs relative to purchased water are significantly less.
• Conservation of water will necessarily increase the impact of the less 
expensive groundwater on the overall cost of the water supply leading to 
greater savings overall.
• Conservation of water will directly impact the most expensive and least 
avoidable cost component of the system, sewerage costs, as those costs 
are based on volume of sewerage pumped into the Savannah system.
• Reducing compostables entering the sewers will reduce volume leading 
to direct savings and reduce damage to the system leading to long term 
savings in repairs and maintenance.

Rational Rate Structuring
• Revisit our current rates considering an increased number of usage steps and an 
aggressive rate structure that will adequately fund the portion of the 
operational budget as well as the infrastructure improvement budget in 
addition to completely covering the cost of purchased water and sewer 
services.
• Create an annual water usage budget based upon the prior two or three years 
of actual usage so that we might have a month-to-month average cost of 
production and purchase of water and sewer services.
• Based upon the average month to month costs, create a pass-through billing 
mechanism for those months where the actual costs are greater than the 
average month to month costs. This can be adjusted upward or downward 
based upon actual costs. The pass-through mechanism can be based upon 25% 
of the outstanding overage for any month and distributed and billed based 
upon the individual rates for each customer. In this manner, the costs of supply 
can be reflected in a real-time billing structure that maintains fiscal integrity.

Rational Conservation Implementation
• Establish a conservation friendly base rate for those consumers whose 
properties can be shown to have Conservation Friendly infrastructure including 
low flow toilets, low flow faucets and shallow wells for landscape watering. A 
system of multi-stepped credits can be offered based on the degree of the 
infrastructure requirements met, for example….
1. Low-volume  Toilets
60% of available credit
2. Low Flow Faucets
20% of available credit
3. Shallow Wells
20% of available credit
• Make changes to the Town Ordinances requiring that all rental properties meet 
the Conservation Friendly standards OR institute separate rate structures for 
owner-occupied vs rental properties to include STR and LTR units.
• Require that all businesses in Thunderbolt achieve Conservation Friendly status 
as a component of their renewal of their business licenses.
