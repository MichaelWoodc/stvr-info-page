a) Water & Sewer Committee – Council Member Drohan 
 
What has happened with the Citizens’ Conservation Committee and ad hoc Committee for Water and Sewer activities 
over the past 6 weeks. 
- Since the Blessing of the Fleet Event, the membership of the CCC has expanded to a total of 16 members. At this 
point there is but one member of the Town Council with committee membership. The CCC had its own float in the 
parade and received a good bit of attention! 
- On June 6th there was a Zoom meeting of the CCC with 5 attendees. These individuals have expressed individual 
interests in expanding the development of shallow wells, graywater systems and municipal electrification/renewable 
energy, expanding the options for recycling and composting as well as expanding options for water conservation 
through, Town and individual infrastructure, Town policy and continuing emphasis on proper pricing of this limited 
resource.  

 
- As issues being discussed among member of the CCC are consistent with the interests of the ad hoc Committee for 
Water and Sewer, members of the CCC were encouraged to bring their concerns regarding these issues to the newly 
established Town Hall Discussion period from 6-6:30 on the 2nd Wednesday of each month prior to the Town Council 
Meeting. 
- Members of the Council were invited to participate in a webinar on May 28th regarding the future and economics of 
municipal electrification. A member of the ad hoc Committee did participate and shared notes (attached) from that 
meeting with the other members of Town Council and the Town Administrator in anticipation of discussions regarding 
vehicle replacement and renewable energy as a viable option for the Town of Thunderbolt.  
- Information regarding the development of “Sustainable Thunderbolt”, in line with other small municipalities, was 
shared with the members of Council earlier this month. The website for “Sustainable Brookhaven” was shared with 
members of Council for their consideration.      https://www.brookhavenga.gov/sustainablebrookhaven 
Composting in Thunderbolt  
On June 22nd, there was a meeting established by the Town Administrator with COR, the organization that helped start 
the small but successful composting option in Thunderbolt at their cost and with the support of the Wesley Oak Church 
who offered the site for the green drop off bin and the administrative support of the Town of Thunderbolt. Thousands 
of pounds of Thunderbolt waste and many cubic feet of volume that would have been part of the weekly home pickup 
by Atlantic Waste otherwise destined for the landfill and inevitable creation of methane gas has been transitioned to 
functional compost by COR.  
This meeting created the opportunity to discuss the expansion of this program with the financial support of the Town 
of Thunderbolt through the conservation line of the Water & Sewer budget. Based on national averages, much of this 
volume would have otherwise become sewage volume and as confirmed by our Public Works Administrator, put 
additional destructive stress on that extremely costly system, reducing costs to the Town through reduced decreased 
payments to the City of Savannah and helping to lengthen the life of our current infrastructure. 
A two phased proposal is being currently developed to - 
1-  Expand the number of consolidated weekly pickup locations to 3 or 4 in convenient locations throughout the Town 
and 
2- Initiate a trial of home pickups utilizing approximately 50 homes with pickups to coincide with landfill pickups 
days. 
Data collection from both of these initiatives will be collected by COR over a 3-4 month period and shared with the 
Town Administrator and the Town Council to determine the most beneficial path to follow going forward.

Thunderbolt Internal Staff Modifications 
 
After meeting various times with Town staff, Finance, and the CPA firm, the finding of support 
for the staff modifications are within budget and will provide a benefit to the Town.  
 
Staff Modifications recommended for approval are… 
 
Promotion – One Police Sgt. To Police Lt. – not only does the Sgt. Currently serve as a Sgt., and 
oversee a duty shift of officers, he often times acts as lead officer on investigations, and in 
keeping with the 2022 Goal of a vetted and capable number two individual in each department 
this individual has and is capable and ready to act in the stead of the Police Chief. This involved 
learning and handling more of the administrative side of police work such as employee issues, 
payroll, state and federal compliance etc… 
Budget impact is a 5% raise from previous rank (change in status) or 5% above highest paid 
subordinate.  
 
Promotion – One patrol officer to Corporal.  
Budget impact is a 5% raise from officer pay (change in status) or 3% above highest paid 
subordinate. 
 
Additional Full Time Employee – The full-time appointment of Ms. Liz Rhoades (Special Projects 
Coordinator) from part-time to full time.  Police had a full-time administrative assistant 
transition to part-time, and this would be a swap of pay and benefits. Concurrently, part of this 
position will be funded from the water and sewer fund as she will assist with the utility billing 
desk, and much of her work on projects is relative to infrastructure.  The water and sewer 
budget also saw a transition as one full time employee departed and was replaced with a part-
time employee.  
Budget impact – none

Thunderbolt Development and  
Resource Utilization Project 
 
 
The recent requests for variances on property development have 
highlighted a notable problem with regards to the upper limits of sewer 
resources allowed us by our contract with the City of Savannah and the 
clear messages from them that those limits are unlikely to be increased. 
Though these issues have been focused upon the 7-acre tract along 
Bonaventure Avenue, I believe that this is in fact a town wide issue and 
deserves to be addressed by then Council in the near term. 
In pursuit of that goal, I have asked our folks in administration to create 
a listing of undeveloped/underdeveloped properties in Thunderbolt so 
that we can then approach the likely future demand of development in 
Thunderbolt relative to the realities of our limited capacities in a 
manner that is responsible to our citizens and equally respects the 
rights of all property owners in Thunderbolt. 
To that end, I have formulated some “Development Zones” that I think 
reasonably identifies the category and location of properties whose 
development will likely place demands on our infrastructure. 
Furthermore, I have taken a stab at identifying “Development Factors” 
that should reasonably be assessed for each of these categories. 
The goal of course would be to operationalize a clear understanding of 
the nature and degree of future development demands and to assess 
those alongside the realities of our limited infrastructure capacities. 
Please review the following information and feel free to help me create 
a more thorough list of Development Zones and Development Factors 
as well as recommending additional factors relative to this issue that 
you believe are important.
